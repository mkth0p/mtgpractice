# Structure of the major adult intelligence batteries — reference for building original practice tasks

Compiled 2026-10-18 for the IQ-training website and the before/after training paper.

**Ground rules for this document**

- It describes **formats, rules and published parameters only**. It reproduces no real test items, stimulus content, answer keys or norm tables. Practice items built from it must be original.
- Every number has a source URL. Anything I could not confirm in a public source is marked **unconfirmed**. Where sources disagree, both are shown.
- Several WAIS-IV administration figures come from university practicum slides, a doctoral dissertation and NIH data dictionaries that paraphrase the Pearson manual. They are secondary sources. Check against the *WAIS-IV Administration and Scoring Manual* (Wechsler, 2008) and the *WAIS-5 Administration and Scoring Manual* (2024) before quoting them in the paper.
- **Ethics and validity note for the paper.** Commercial batteries (WAIS, SB5, WASI-II, Raven's) are secure tests. Training on lookalike items can produce practice and coaching gains that inflate post-test scores without any change in ability. The WAIS-5 retest gain alone is about 3.7 FSIQ points after roughly a month (see §1.8). Your design should include a control group, ideally an active one, and should report gains on tasks *not* trained (far transfer). Professional standards also restrict releasing or imitating secure items, which is one more reason to keep practice items clearly original.

## 0. Quick reference: parameters for task builders

| Task family | Battery source | Key published parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Block-design-style construction | WAIS-IV BD | 14 items. 2-block (sample, items 1–2), 4-block (items 3–10), 9-block (items 11–14). Time limits: items 1–4 **30 s**, items 5–10 **60 s**, items 11–14 **120 s**. Start at item 5. Reverse if item 5 or 6 is not perfect. **Discontinue after 2 consecutive 0s.** Time bonus on items 9–14 (4–7 points). Raw score 0–66. |
| Visual Puzzles (choose 3 of 6 pieces) | WAIS-IV VP | 26 items. Items 1–7 **20 s**, items 8–26 **30 s**. Start at item 5. Discontinue after 3 consecutive 0s. All 3 pieces must be correct for credit. Prompt "Do you have an answer?" with 10 s left. |
| Figure Weights (balance scales) | WAIS-IV FW | Items 1–12 **20 s**, items 13+ **40 s**. Start at item 4. Reverse if item 4 or 5 is not perfect. Discontinue after 3 consecutive 0s. Ages 16–69 only on the WAIS-IV. |
| Matrix Reasoning | WAIS-IV / WAIS-5 MR | WAIS-IV: 26 items, 0/1 scoring, start at item 4, discontinue after 3 consecutive 0s, **no strict limit (≈30 s guideline)**. WAIS-5: 26 items (secondary source). |
| Mental arithmetic word problems | WAIS-IV AR | 22 items, **30 s per item**, 0/1 scoring. Start at item 6. Discontinue after 3 consecutive 0s. Repeat a problem once, with the clock still running. No paper. |
| Digit span F / B / Sequencing | WAIS-IV DS | 8 items × 2 trials per task. Forward lengths 2–9. **1 digit per second**, no repeats. Discontinue a task when **both trials of an item are failed**. Each task scores 0–16; DS total 0–48. |
| Letter-Number Sequencing | WAIS-IV LN | 3 trials per item. Discontinue when all 3 trials of an item score 0. Numbers ascending, then letters alphabetically. |
| Coding (digit to symbol) | WAIS-IV/5 CD | **120 s**. 135 items (raw 0–135). No skipping. |
| Symbol Search | WAIS-IV/5 SS | **120 s**. 60 items (raw 0–60). Score is correct minus incorrect (standard Wechsler rule; see §1.4). |
| Raven-style 3×3 matrices | Raven SPM / APM | SPM: 60 items in 5 sets (A–E) of 12. APM: Set I 12 + Set II 36. APM commonly **40 min** (or untimed). Raven's 2: 48 items in 45 min for ages 9–79; digital short form 24 items in 20 min. |
| Matrix rule taxonomy | Carpenter, Just & Shell (1990) | 5 rules: constant in a row; quantitative pairwise progression; figure addition/subtraction; distribution of three values; distribution of two values. Rule count explained **57%** of variance in APM error rates. |
| Public-domain item types | ICAR | Letter-number series (9), matrix reasoning (11, 3×3, 6 options), verbal reasoning (16), 3-D cube rotation (24). Untimed. ICAR16 α = .81. |
| Culture Fair (CFIT) Scale 3 | Cattell | Series 13 items / 3 min; Classification 14 / 4 min; Matrices 13 / 3 min; Conditions 10 / 2.5 min. 50 items in 12.5 min (secondary source). |
| Wonderlic | WPT-R | 50 items in 12 min; quick form 30 items in 8 min. Mixed verbal, numeric and logic items. |

**Ancillary indexes (15).** Some Pearson FAQ text says 14, so the count is flagged on the product page; Canivez et al. confirm 5 + 6 + 4 = 15. Composition from the AHEAD 2025 Pearson-based presentation; an asterisk marks a secondary subtest:

- *Summary (4):* GAI = SI, VC, BD, FW, MR. CPI = DQ, RD, CD, SS. Nonverbal (NVI) = BD, VP, FW, MR, CD, SSP*. Nonmotor (NMI) = SI, VC, VP, FW, MR, DQ*, NSQ*.
- *Domain-specific (6):* Verbal Reasoning (SI, CO*, SR*); Quantitative Reasoning (FW, AR*); Visual WM (SSP, SA); Auditory WM–Registration (RD, DF*); Auditory WM–Manipulation (DQ, DB*, LN*); Motor-Reduced PS (SS, NSQ*).

**Substitution.** One substitution is allowed in the FSIQ, from the same domain. Per the secondary source, none is allowed in primary or ancillary indexes except the NMI ([acisiq](https://acisiq.com/wais-5-subtests)); unconfirmed against the manual.

### 1.3 WAIS-5 administration rules (start, reverse, discontinue, time limits)

- **Not available in public sources.** Pearson publishes only total testing times. Per-item time limits, start points and discontinue rules for the WAIS-5 are **unconfirmed**.
- Publicly stated: timed subtests are BD, VP, FW, AR, CD, SS and NSQ; MR has a ~30-s guideline (secondary, [acisiq](https://acisiq.com/wais-5-subtests)); Digits Forward must be given before Digit Sequencing.
- A Pearson-based training deck counts "52 commonly made errors" and "471 scores to enter on record form" ([AHEAD 2025](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/AHEAD/38b602f4-ec53-451c-9be0-5c0bf5d27c0a/UploadedImages/CONFERNCES/2025_AHEAD/2025_HANDOUTS/10.09/10_09_Meet_the_WAIS5_An_Overview_for_Disability_Professionals.pdf)).
- **Practical recommendation:** for practice tasks in retained subtests (BD, VP, FW, AR, CD, SS, MR), use the WAIS-IV timings in §2 as the model and label them "WAIS-IV-style timing." Whether the WAIS-5 kept those timings is **unconfirmed**.

### 1.4 Score conversion

- Raw score → **age-referenced scaled score (M = 10, SD = 3, range 1–19)** via norm tables by age group. Sources: Canivez et al. 2026; [WAIS-5 sample report](https://pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-5/wais-5-sample-score-report.pdf).
- Sum of the relevant scaled scores → **composite (index, FSIQ) standard score (M = 100, SD = 15)** via sum-of-scaled-scores tables. The FSIQ uses the sum of 7 scaled scores; each primary index uses 2.
- A Pearson report option gives **reference-group scaled scores** based on ages 20:0–34:11 ([acisiq](https://acisiq.com/wais-5-subtests)). A separate report gives demographically referenced scores ([sample](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-5/wais-5-sample-score-report-with-demographically-referenced-scores.pdf)).
- The FSIQ weights higher-order abilities (VC, VS, FR: 5 of 7 subtests) more heavily than WM and PS (AHEAD 2025).

### 1.5 Reliability and SEM

- **FSIQ mean reliability .97** (Winter, Trudel & Kaufman 2024, *J. Intelligence* 12(11):118, as summarized by [acisiq](https://acisiq.com/wais-5-subtests) and [iqmetrics](https://iqmetrics.org/news/wais-iv-and-wais-5-index-scores-explained/)). Subtest internal consistency uses split-half coefficients, except speeded subtests, which use test–retest ([Canivez et al. 2026](https://ux1.eiu.edu/~glcanivez/Adobe%20pdf/Publications-papers/Canivez%20et%20al.%20(2026,%20February)%20Construct%20Validity%20of%20the%20WAIS-5%20(with%20Appendix).pdf)). Coefficients for individual subtests and indexes: **unconfirmed** (not in public sources).
- **Test–retest:** mean interval 29 days (range 12–124). FSIQ stability .93 (n = 201). Gains: **+3.7 FSIQ points** at ages 16–34 and 35–69; +2.3 at ages 70–90 (acisiq summary of Pearson data). The WAIS-IV comparison figure is 4.5–5 points (iqmetrics summary of Winter et al.). **Use these as the expected pure-retest gain in the paper's control condition.**
- **SEMs (sample report)** in scaled-score points: SI 1.34, VC 0.85, IN 0.90, CO 0.99, BD 1.31, VP 0.99, MR 0.85, FW 0.73, AR 0.90, SR 0.67, DQ 1.12, RD 0.79, DF 1.12, DB 0.79, LN 1.41, SSP 1.12, SA 0.90, CD 1.31, SS 1.47, NSQ 1.24. Composite SEMs in that report: FSIQ 3.00, VCI 4.50, VSI 4.74, FRI 3.00, WMI 4.50, PSI 5.61, GAI 3.35, NVI 3.00. Source: [WAIS-5 sample score report](https://pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-5/wais-5-sample-score-report.pdf).
- **Confidence intervals:** the report prints **95% CIs computed with the Standard Error of Estimation**, centred on the estimated true score, so they are asymmetric around the obtained score. Example: FSIQ 111 → 106–116; VCI 124 → 115–130. Critical values are given at p = .05; base rates are reported by ability level (sample report).
- **Comparison with WAIS-IV:** n = 186, counterbalanced. Mean FSIQ 101.6 (WAIS-IV) vs 99.7 (WAIS-5), r = .92 (iqmetrics summary of Winter et al. 2024).

### 1.6 Factor structure (independent analysis)

Canivez et al. (2026) reanalysed the manual's summary statistics (N = 2,020). Exploratory analysis supported **4 factors, not 5**: VS and FR merged into one perceptual reasoning factor. In the bifactor model, ωH(g) = .887 and group-factor ωHS = .069–.361. *g* accounted for 72% of common variance. Implication for the paper: use the FSIQ (or GAI) as the primary outcome. Index-level change scores carry little unique reliable variance. [Source](https://ux1.eiu.edu/~glcanivez/Adobe%20pdf/Publications-papers/Canivez%20et%20al.%20(2026,%20February)%20Construct%20Validity%20of%20the%20WAIS-5%20(with%20Appendix).pdf).

### 1.7 Descriptive classification (WAIS-5)

The sample reports use **Very high / Above average / Average / Below average / Very low**. Pearson does not publish the cut-offs in public materials and calls descriptors "only suggestions." The ranges below are **inferred** from printed scores (iqmetrics): Very high ≥120; Above average 110–119; Average 90–109; Below average 80–89; Very low ≤79. Confirmed examples from the sample report: 124 = Very high, 111 and 115 = Above average, 97–109 = Average ([sample report](https://pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-5/wais-5-sample-score-report.pdf); [iqmetrics](https://iqmetrics.org/news/wais-iv-and-wais-5-index-scores-explained/)). Whether the WAIS-5 manual adds an "Extremely high/low" band at ≥130 / ≤69 is **unconfirmed**. Scaled-score average range: 8–12; standard-score average range: 90–110 (AHEAD 2025).

### 1.8 Implications for the training study

- Expect about **+3.7 FSIQ points** from retest alone at ~1 month (WAIS-5), and more on the WAIS-IV.
- Untimed-reasoning and speed subtests are differentially sensitive to practice. On the WAIS-IV, practice effects were largest on PRI subtests ([Canivez 2010 Buros review](https://www.ux1.eiu.edu/~glcanivez/Adobe%20pdf/Publications-Papers/Canivez%20(2010)%20Buros%20MMY%20WAIS-IV%20Review.pdf)).

### 2.3 Per-subtest administration (format, items, timing, start, reverse, discontinue)

Sources: **[WKU] =** [WKU IQ practicum slides, part 1](https://people.wku.edu/rick.grieve/IQPracticum/PowerPoint/WAIS%20PP%20One.pdf) and [part 2](https://people.wku.edu/rick.grieve/IQPracticum/PowerPoint/WAIS%20PP%20Two.pdf). **[NSU] =** [Nova Southeastern dissertation](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1090&context=cps_stuetd). **[FITBIR] =** NIH FITBIR data dictionary for [BD time](https://fitbir.nih.gov/dictionary/publicData/dataElementAction!view.action?dataElementName=WAISIVBDTimeDur&publicArea=true&style.key=fitbir-style) and [BD items 5–8](https://fitbir.nih.gov/dictionary/publicData/dataElementAction!view.action?dataElementName=WAISIVBDItem5to8Score&publicArea=true&style.key=fitbir-style). **[SR] =** [Pearson WAIS-IV sample score report](https://www.pearsonclinical.asia/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-iv/wais-iv-score-report.pdf), used for raw-score ranges.

**General Wechsler rules**

- Each subtest has an age-based start point. Examinees suspected of intellectual disability start at item 1.
- If either of the first two start-point items is not perfect, give the preceding items in reverse order until 2 consecutive perfect scores (basal = 2). Earlier items then receive full credit.
- Each subtest has a fixed discontinue rule. Sources: [WKU]; [NSU p. 19]; [prezi summary](https://prezi.com/gixrv3ikkw_m/wechsler-adult-intelligence-scale-fourth-edition-wais-iv).

### 2.4 Score conversion, SEM and CI (WAIS-IV)

- Raw score → age-based scaled score (M 10, SD 3) → sum of scaled scores → composite (M 100, SD 15) [Canivez 2010].
- Sample-report subtest SEMs: SI 0.95, VC 0.67, IN 0.73, CO 1.27, BD 0.99, MR 0.90, VP 0.99, DS 0.73, AR 1.20, SS 1.12, CD 1.12. Process-score SEMs: DSF 1.04, DSB 1.37, DSS 1.12 [SR].
- Composite SEMs from a Pearson GAI symposium, as reported by [acisiq](https://acisiq.com/wais-iv): VCI 2.85, PRI 3.48, WMI 3.67, PSI 4.78, GAI 2.57. FSIQ SEM: **unconfirmed** (≈2.2 if r = .98, by formula).
- The report prints 95% CIs "based on overall average SEMs" [SR].
- **WAIS-IV descriptors (traditional Wechsler bands)**, per [iqmetrics](https://iqmetrics.org/news/wais-iv-and-wais-5-index-scores-explained/): Very Superior ≥130; Superior 120–129; High Average 110–119; Average 90–109; Low Average 80–89; Borderline 70–79; Extremely Low ≤69. The sample report confirms "Superior" for 123 and "Very Superior" for 133 and 139 [SR].

### 2.5 Generic psychometric formulas for your scoring engine (standard theory, not battery-specific)

- SEM = SD·√(1 − r_xx). For example, r = .97 and SD 15 give SEM ≈ 2.6. A 95% CI on the obtained score is X ± 1.96·SEM.
- Estimated true score T′ = M + r_xx(X − M). SEE = SD·√(r_xx(1 − r_xx)). Pearson's WAIS-5 CIs centre on T′ and use the SEE.
- Reliable Change Index (Jacobson & Truax): RCI = (X₂ − X₁) / (√2·SEM). For training studies, also subtract the mean practice effect, about 3.7 FSIQ points for the WAIS-5.
- Scaled score z = (SS − 10)/3; standard score z = (SS − 100)/15. Percentile = Φ(z).

### 3.2 Carpenter, Just & Shell (1990) rule taxonomy — the core for generating items

Source: Carpenter, P. A., Just, M. A., & Shell, P. (1990). What one intelligence test measures: A theoretical account of the processing in the Raven Progressive Matrices Test. *Psychological Review, 97*(3), 404–431. CMU technical report version: [DTIC ADA221448](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA221448.pdf).

Rules apply to **attributes** of the figures in each cell (shape type, size, number, position, orientation, shading/texture, line type) and operate along **rows**. Columns usually follow too.

| # | Rule (Carpenter name) | Definition (paraphrased from Table 1) | Generator recipe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Constant in a row** | The same value occurs throughout a row but changes down a column. | Attribute value v(row) is fixed across the columns of a row; pick a different value per row. |
| 2 | **Quantitative pairwise progression** | A quantitative increment or decrement between adjacent entries in an attribute such as size, position or number. | v(r,c) = base(r) + c·Δ. Δ is constant across the matrix (e.g., +1 dot, +45° rotation, size step). |
| 3 | **Figure addition or subtraction** | A figure from one column is added to (juxtaposed or superimposed on) another, or subtracted from it, to produce the third. | Cell3 = Cell1 ∪ Cell2 (addition) or Cell1 \ Cell2 (subtraction), element-wise on line segments or sub-figures. |
| 4 | **Distribution of three values** | Three values of a categorical attribute (e.g., figure type) are distributed through a row; each appears once per row (Latin-square pattern). | Choose 3 values {a, b, c}; each row is a permutation; the 3×3 grid forms a Latin square. |
| 5 | **Distribution of two values** | Two values of a categorical attribute are distributed through a row, and the third value is null. | Each element appears in exactly 2 of the 3 cells of a row. Equivalent to XOR / "addition modulo 2" (the authors note this re-description). |

**Points from the paper that matter for item generation and difficulty**

- The rule-induction priority in the BETTERAVEN model was constant → progression → distribution of three → addition/subtraction → distribution of two. In human verbal protocols, quantitative progression was induced before distribution of two in 83% of observations, so distribution of two is the hardest rule to discover.
- **Rule count drives difficulty.** Across 32 classifiable APM problems, the number of rule tokens accounted for **57% of the variance in mean error rates** (45% if constant rules are excluded). Mean error rates were **16% for 1-token problems vs 59% for 3–4-token problems**. Single-token error rates by type: pairwise progression 6%, addition/subtraction 17%, distribution of three 29%. RT and error rate correlated r = .87.
- **Correspondence finding**, i.e. deciding which elements correspond across cells, is a major independent source of difficulty. In problems with misleading cues, faulty correspondences appeared in verbal reports for 74% of errors. Generators can raise difficulty by making corresponding elements differ in surface features or by adding distractor elements.
- **Goal management / working memory:** Tower of Hanoi errors correlated r = .77 with Raven errors (n = 45). FAIRAVEN (121 productions) matched the median college student. BETTERAVEN added a goal monitor and solved all classifiable items.

### 3.3 Automatic matrix item generation (AIG) and difficulty models

The overview is drawn mainly from the review by [Yang et al., "Automatic Item Generation of Figural Analogy Problems: A Review and Outlook" (arXiv 2201.08450)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08450).

| Approach | Key features | Difficulty findings |
|---|---|---|
| **Hornke & Habon (1986)** | 648 3×3 items generated systematically. 8 variation rules (identity, addition, subtraction, intersection, XOR, orientation, progression, open/closed shape). Analogical direction: row or column. Perceptual organization: separation, integration, embedding. At most 2 rules per item. | These dimensions explained **≈40%** of difficulty. The number of elements and rules is a further source of difficulty. |
| **Embretson's Cognitive Design System (1995, 1998, 2004)**; *Psychological Methods 3*(3), 1998, "A cognitive design system approach to generating valid tests: Application to abstract reasoning" | Builds on Carpenter's model (number and level of rules = working-memory load) and adds 3 binary perceptual features: **object overlay, object fusion, object distortion**. Item difficulty and discrimination are modelled as functions of design features (LLTM/2PL-constrained), then items are generated to target difficulty. Software: ITEMGEN. | Hand-made items can have attrition up to 50% (Embretson 2004, via review). The exact R² of Embretson's 1998 model is **unconfirmed** here. Luo & Yang 2024 ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1353419/pdf)) simulate CDS item banks and find that design features need R² ≥ 75% to keep a 30-item CAT as efficient as a calibrated bank. |
| **Primi (2001)** | *Harmonic* vs *non-harmonic* items. In non-harmonic items, the visual Gestalt groupings conflict with the rule structure. | Non-harmonic items are harder (extra effort to resolve competing groupings). |
| **Arendasy (2002), GeomGen** | Perceptual organization: "classical view" (appearance changes; number and position fixed) vs "normal view" (number and position change). | – |
| **Matzen et al. (2010), Sandia Matrix Generator.** *Behav. Res. Methods 42*(2), 525–541, [doi:10.3758/BRM.42.2.525](https://doi.org/10.3758/BRM.42.2.525); code and normed stimuli at [github.com/sandialabs/Matrices](https://github.com/sandialabs/Matrices) | Recreates SPM-like 3×3 items. Two item types never mixed: (a) **element transformation** (progressions in shape, shading, orientation, size, number; one or more directions); (b) **logic** (AND, OR, XOR, add/subtract of overlaid elements across a row). Distractors come from 6 strategies (matrix entries, transformed correct/incorrect answers, novel feature combinations). | 840 generated items were normed. Their difficulty was "generally very similar" to SPM items. Difficulty was driven mainly by **number of rules, analogical directions, and problem type**. |
| **Hofer (2004), MatrixDeveloper** | 4×4 matrices, 5 rules, plus overlap/fusion/distortion | – |
| **Wang & Su (2015)** | First-order-logic generator for 3×3 items | Difficulty similar to APM. Adding distracting attributes greatly increased difficulty. |
| **Blum & Holling (2018), IMak R package** | 2×2 items, 5 rule types. Answer set includes "no correct answer" and "I don't know." | Number and type of rules only partly predicted difficulty (23 items, n = 307). |
| **PGM** (Barrett et al. 2018), **RAVEN** (Zhang et al. 2019), **I-RAVEN** (Hu et al. 2021), **RAVEN-FAIR** (Benny et al. 2021) | ML benchmark generators | **Warning for distractor design:** in RAVEN each distractor changes one attribute of the correct answer, so the correct answer is the "modal" option. Models solved 70–90%+ of items from the answer set alone, without seeing the matrix. I-RAVEN builds answer sets as a 3-regular graph, which brings context-blind accuracy below 20% (chance 12.5%). |

**Recommended item-generation spec for the training site** (derived from the above)

1. **Grid:** 3×3 with the bottom-right cell missing; 8 options (or 6 for easy items).
2. **Attributes:** shape type, count (1–9), size (3–5 levels), rotation (45° steps), fill (empty/striped/solid), position in cell, line style.
3. **Rules per attribute:** constant, progression (±Δ), distribution of three (Latin square), distribution of two (XOR), addition/subtraction (union/difference of line segments).
4. **Difficulty knobs, roughly in order of effect:**
   - number of rule tokens (1 → 4+);
   - rule type (progression easiest; distribution of two hardest);
   - number of elements per cell;
   - correspondence ambiguity, overlay, fusion or distortion;
   - non-harmonic perceptual grouping;
   - column-wise or two-direction rules;
   - irrelevant varying attributes as noise.
5. **Distractors:** build them as in I-RAVEN, as a tree or graph of single-attribute modifications away from *several* nodes, not only from the answer, so the correct answer is not the modal option. Check that no option can be identified without the matrix (test with a context-blind heuristic solver).
6. **Calibration:** pre-assign predicted difficulty from rule count and type, then recalibrate with Rasch/2PL on pilot data. Treat rule count alone as explaining roughly 40–60% of difficulty variance (Hornke & Habon ≈40%; Carpenter 57%).

## 10. What processing-speed tasks look like

- **Coding / digit-symbol:** a key pairs 9 digits with 9 abstract symbols; copy symbols under a long row of digits for 120 s (WAIS: 135 cells). Score = correct cells.
- **Symbol Search:** 1–2 target symbols and a search group; mark whether a target is present, as many rows as possible in 120 s (WAIS: 60 rows). Score = correct − incorrect.
- **Cancellation (WAIS-IV):** strike target shape–colour combinations in a structured array under a time limit.
- **Naming Speed Quantity (WAIS-5):** rapid oral naming of the number of squares in each box. Motor-free. Score is completion time and errors (**unconfirmed**).
- All are simple tasks scored on speed. They are the least *g*-loaded group factor: PSI ωHS .361 on the WAIS-5 is the largest unique group variance, but its *g* loading is lowest (Canivez et al. 2026). Practice gains on them come largely from familiarity with the task. Report them separately in the paper.
