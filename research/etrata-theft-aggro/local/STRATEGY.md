# How to play Etrata as a high Bracket 4 aggro deck: the strategy behind the versions

Written 2026-10-05 after the user stopped the card-by-card search ("we should be checking the best overall way to play this deck as a high bracket 4 aggro"). This file collects what the research established and turns it into design rules; the versions in `lists/` and the bot brain in `miku/game/decks-heist.js` follow it. Sources: `../sources/bracket4-deckbuilding.md`, `../sources/bracket4-piloting-and-aggro.md`, `../sources/etrata-strategy.md`, `../sources/public-lists.md`, `../sources/heist-research.md`.

## 1. What Bracket 4 means (official)
Wizards' Commander Brackets update of 21 October 2025 (magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/commander-brackets-beta-update-october-21-2025):
- **Bracket 4, Optimized:** decks are expected "to be lethal, consistent, and fast, designed to take people down as fast as possible". Game Changers "are likely to be fast mana, snowballing resource engines, free disruption, and tutors". Win conditions are "efficient and instantaneous". "Generally, you should expect to play at least four turns before you win or lose."
- **Bracket 3, Upgraded:** at least six turns; decks that "can effectively disrupt opponents" but accumulate resources through "many proactive and reactive plays".
- **Bracket 5, cEDH:** built for the cEDH metagame; "games could end on any turn". Bracket 4 is explicitly *not* that: the best version of a deck you want to play, not the deck that wins the tournament.
- No deckbuilding limits at Bracket 4 beyond the banned list: unlimited Game Changers, two-card combos, extra turns, mass land denial. The Game Changers list of that update (53 cards) includes, in our colors: Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Imperial Seal, Mystical Tutor, Gifts Ungiven, Intuition, Ad Nauseam, Bolas's Citadel, Braids, Necropotence, Opposition Agent, Orcish Bowmasters, Tergrid, Thassa's Oracle, Consecrated Sphinx, Cyclonic Rift, Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Narset, Rhystic Study, Notion Thief, Drannith Magistrate, Underworld Breach, Ancient Tomb, Chrome Mox, Grim Monolith, Lion's Eye Diamond, Mana Vault, Mox Diamond, The One Ring, Panoptic Mirror, Field of the Dead, Glacial Chasm, Mishra's Workshop, The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale. (Yuriko, Kinnan, Winota, Deflecting Swat and Food Chain were removed from the list in that update.)

**Consequence for this deck:** "high Bracket 4 aggro" is a deck built to present a kill by turns 4–6 with redundancy, backed by fast mana, tutors and free interaction, not a deck that grinds value from stolen cards over twelve rounds. Every version below is judged by how early and how reliably it kills, and by what it does when the first plan is answered.

## 2. What the engine measures (the opponents)
- The precon bots (Ghired, Isperia, Kaalia, Lathril, Wilhelt) lose around round 11–12 to the current lists; the Bracket 4 bots (Azusa, Corrupted Etrata, Edgar, Etrata B4, Ghalta, Krenko, Talrand, Ur-Dragon) end games around round 7–8. Ghalta, the engine's best bot against precons (56%), deals 145 combat damage a game with a board of 23 power by round 8; the Etrata lists so far deal about 36 and have 17 power on board by round 8 (`EXPERIMENTS.md` #8 and the skA traces).
- The hero's life in the aggro lists falls from 40 to about 20 by round 8 and 15 by round 12 (trace tables). The deck doesn't block, and the precon bots attack it freely.
- Ramses decides games: 50% wins when he lands, 10% when he doesn't; he lands in half the games and is removed in half the losses where he landed. Etrata matters too: never casting her costs 11–14 points.

## 3. What real pilots do (the data collected so far)
- **EDHREC, Etrata decks tagged Bracket 4 (42 decks, `public-lists.md` §1.4):** Ramses 83%, Maskwood Nexus 81% (+21 points over all Etrata decks), Roaming Throne 60% (+28), Vein Ripper 50% (+29), Spark Double 48% (+26), Sakashima of a Thousand Faces 36% (+36); the Bracket 4 shell: Demonic Tutor 71%, Cyclonic Rift 69%, Fierce Guardianship 67%, Vampiric Tutor 62%, Mana Drain 60%, Rhystic Study 60%, Deadly Rollick 57%, Dark Ritual 50%, Force of Negation 33%, Force of Will 31%, Diabolic Intent 33%, Reanimate 38%.
- **The Fugitives primer (ozmly, Bracket 4):** cheap Assassins and fast mana, Etrata as early as possible, evasion (Cover of Darkness), *copy Etrata* (Auton Soldier with myriad, Sakashimas, Spark Double) so every hit cloaks several times, draw on combat damage, then make the whole board of 2/2s Assassins (Maskwood, Arcane Adaptation, Conspiracy) so each one that connects steals again. Closers named: the board itself, decking opponents by cloaking their libraries, or Vein Ripper with an altar. 33 lands with fast mana; "about 34 lands and more two-drops" without it. Thieving Amalgam, Agent of Treachery, Titan of Littjara and Tasha were cut as too slow.
- **One pilot's real record** (ShoMinamoto's Assassin typal value list): 7 wins in 27 games, about the fair share of 25%.
- **The Yuriko template** (EDHREC "tempo" theme, 610 decks; the learncedh cEDH page; an Archidekt "High Power Bracket 4 Tempo" list with 30 lands, 13 one-mana evasive creatures, 13 ninjas and **21 instants, nearly all free**: Force of Will, Force of Negation, Fierce Guardianship, Deadly Rollick, Snuff Out, Force of Despair, Flare of Denial, Flare of Malice, Misdirection, Commandeer, Submerge, Subtlety, Dismember, An Offer You Can't Refuse): the Dimir aggro shell that works at high power is **cheap evasive bodies + ninjutsu + free interaction + top-deck manipulation**, with the commander providing the damage multiplier. Its weaknesses, per the "how to beat Yuriko" article (Levi Perry, 2025-02-26): bigger creatures that block 1/1 fliers, removal on the commander, and pressure in the mid game once the removal runs out.
