# How to play Etrata as a high Bracket 4 aggro deck: the strategy behind the versions

Written 2026-10-05 after the user stopped the card-by-card search ("we should be checking the best overall way to play this deck as a high bracket 4 aggro"). This file collects what the research established and turns it into design rules; the versions in `lists/` and the bot brain in `miku/game/decks-heist.js` follow it. Sources: `../sources/bracket4-deckbuilding.md`, `../sources/bracket4-piloting-and-aggro.md`, `../sources/etrata-strategy.md`, `../sources/public-lists.md`, `../sources/heist-research.md`.

## 1. What Bracket 4 means (official)
Wizards' Commander Brackets update of 21 October 2025 (magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/commander-brackets-beta-update-october-21-2025):
- **Bracket 4, Optimized:** decks are expected "to be lethal, consistent, and fast, designed to take people down as fast as possible". Game Changers "are likely to be fast mana, snowballing resource engines, free disruption, and tutors". Win conditions are "efficient and instantaneous". "Generally, you should expect to play at least four turns before you win or lose."
- **Bracket 3, Upgraded:** at least six turns; decks that "can effectively disrupt opponents" but accumulate resources through "many proactive and reactive plays".
- **Bracket 5, cEDH:** built for the cEDH metagame; "games could end on any turn". Bracket 4 is explicitly *not* that: the best version of a deck you want to play, not the deck that wins the tournament.
- No deckbuilding limits at Bracket 4 beyond the banned list: unlimited Game Changers, two-card combos, extra turns, mass land denial. The Game Changers list of that update (53 cards) includes, in our colors: Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Imperial Seal, Mystical Tutor, Gifts Ungiven, Intuition, Ad Nauseam, Bolas's Citadel, Braids, Necropotence, Opposition Agent, Orcish Bowmasters, Tergrid, Thassa's Oracle, Consecrated Sphinx, Cyclonic Rift, Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Narset, Rhystic Study, Notion Thief, Drannith Magistrate, Underworld Breach, Ancient Tomb, Chrome Mox, Grim Monolith, Lion's Eye Diamond, Mana Vault, Mox Diamond, The One Ring, Panoptic Mirror, Field of the Dead, Glacial Chasm, Mishra's Workshop, The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale. (Yuriko, Kinnan, Winota, Deflecting Swat and Food Chain were removed from the list in that update.)

**Consequence for this deck:** "high Bracket 4 aggro" is a deck built to present a kill by turns 4–6 with redundancy, backed by fast mana, tutors and free interaction, not a deck that grinds value from stolen cards over twelve rounds. Every version below is judged by how early and how reliably it kills, and by what it does when the first plan is answered.

## 2. What the engine measures (the opponents)
- The precon bots (Ghired, Isperia, Kaalia, Lathril, Wilhelt) lose around round 11–12 to the current lists; the Bracket 4 bots (Azusa, Corrupted Etrata, Edgar, Etrata B4, Ghalta, Krenko, Talrand, Ur-Dragon) end games around round 7–8. Ghalta, the engine's best bot against precons (56%), deals 145 combat damage a game with a board of 23 power by round 8; the Etrata lists so far deal about 36 and have 17 power on board by round 8 (`EXPERIMENTS.md` #8 and the skA traces).
- The hero's life in the aggro lists falls from 40 to about 20 by round 8 and 15 by round 12 (trace tables). The deck doesn't block, and the precon bots attack it freely.
- Ramses decides games: 50% wins when he lands, 10% when he doesn't; he lands in half the games and is removed in half the losses where he landed. Etrata matters too: never casting her costs 11–14 points.
- **Board wipes are the aggro list's main cause of death against precons** (A2, 2,016 games each, `a2tele-*`): the hero faces 0.69 opposing wipes a game against precons (0.84 in lost games, 0.40 in won ones); 56% of its losses had at least one wipe resolve against it, against 31% of its wins. It loses 5.3 creatures a game on opponents' turns (6.0 in losses) and 2.6 on its own. Against the Bracket 4 bots wipes are rarer (0.29 a game) and the deck simply gets outraced: Corrupted Etrata wins 44% and Ghalta 39% of the games they share with it.
- **The cut sweep of A2** (every nonland card replaced by a basic land, 2,016 paired games per field): only Ramses (−14 / −5.6 points when cut), Bloodletter (−2.9 / −2.3), Sol Ring (−2.4 / −1.4), Demonic Tutor, Imperial Seal and Vampiric Tutor (−1.3 to −1.9) are worth clearly more than a land. Twenty-five cheap Assassins, the anthems, the equipment, the counters and the draw engines each measure within ±1.5 of a land. The deck is a Ramses-and-Bloodletter kill with a filler shell: what matters is finding and resolving the kill (tutors, fast mana, protection, recursion), surviving until then (wipe resilience, blockers, life), and how much the opponents are punished for stopping it (redundant closers).
- Where the damage comes from (A2 vs precons): 35.5 combat, 8.5 from halving triggers, 3.5 drain, 47.5 of the 120 life on the table; the last kill in won games is Ramses' trigger 69% of the time.

## 3. What real pilots do (the data collected so far)
- **EDHREC, Etrata decks tagged Bracket 4 (42 decks, `public-lists.md` §1.4):** Ramses 83%, Maskwood Nexus 81% (+21 points over all Etrata decks), Roaming Throne 60% (+28), Vein Ripper 50% (+29), Spark Double 48% (+26), Sakashima of a Thousand Faces 36% (+36); the Bracket 4 shell: Demonic Tutor 71%, Cyclonic Rift 69%, Fierce Guardianship 67%, Vampiric Tutor 62%, Mana Drain 60%, Rhystic Study 60%, Deadly Rollick 57%, Dark Ritual 50%, Force of Negation 33%, Force of Will 31%, Diabolic Intent 33%, Reanimate 38%.
- **The Fugitives primer (ozmly, Bracket 4):** cheap Assassins and fast mana, Etrata as early as possible, evasion (Cover of Darkness), *copy Etrata* (Auton Soldier with myriad, Sakashimas, Spark Double) so every hit cloaks several times, draw on combat damage, then make the whole board of 2/2s Assassins (Maskwood, Arcane Adaptation, Conspiracy) so each one that connects steals again. Closers named: the board itself, decking opponents by cloaking their libraries, or Vein Ripper with an altar. 33 lands with fast mana; "about 34 lands and more two-drops" without it. Thieving Amalgam, Agent of Treachery, Titan of Littjara and Tasha were cut as too slow.
- **One pilot's real record** (ShoMinamoto's Assassin typal value list): 7 wins in 27 games, about the fair share of 25%.
- **The Yuriko template** (EDHREC "tempo" theme, 610 decks; the learncedh cEDH page; an Archidekt "High Power Bracket 4 Tempo" list with 30 lands, 13 one-mana evasive creatures, 13 ninjas and **21 instants, nearly all free**: Force of Will, Force of Negation, Fierce Guardianship, Deadly Rollick, Snuff Out, Force of Despair, Flare of Denial, Flare of Malice, Misdirection, Commandeer, Submerge, Subtlety, Dismember, An Offer You Can't Refuse): the Dimir aggro shell that works at high power is **cheap evasive bodies + ninjutsu + free interaction + top-deck manipulation**, with the commander providing the damage multiplier. Its weaknesses, per the "how to beat Yuriko" article (Levi Perry, 2025-02-26): bigger creatures that block 1/1 fliers, removal on the commander, and pressure in the mid game once the removal runs out.

## 4. Design rules
From `../sources/bracket4-piloting-and-aggro.md` (65 sources; the numbers in brackets are its source tags) and the facts above. Deckbuilding counts are added from `../sources/bracket4-deckbuilding.md` when it lands.

### Piloting rules the bot follows
1. **Mulligan is the default.** Keep only a hand that does something by turn 2–3: a 0–2 mana evasive creature plus the mana to cast Etrata by turn 3, or fast mana plus a real threat, or a broken turn 1–2 engine (Rhystic Study, Mystic Remora, Necropotence) with lands for it. Interaction alone, or draw without development, is not a keep [S3, S11, S51, S52]. (`b4Mulligan` in `decks-heist.js`, switch `HEIST_ON=mull2` while it's measured.)
2. **Develop first, interact on fundamental turns.** Mana, then the engine pieces; counters are for wipes, for removal aimed at Ramses or Etrata, and for an opponent's actual win attempt, never for a Sol Ring [S3, S4, S11]. Free counters (Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Force of Negation, Flare of Denial, Mana Drain) let the deck tap out for threats and still answer.
3. **Don't overextend into wraths.** Two or three pressure creatures out, the rest in hand to rebuild; prefer noncreature permanents (equipment, enchantments, the typal artifacts) for the rest of the plan [S16, S54]. The telemetry says this is the aggro list's main loss against precons (56% of losses had a wipe).
4. **Pick one player and kill them.** Voltron and Assassin decks eliminate one player at a time, which with Ramses is the whole game [S27, S1]; the mark is the opponent the current board kills soonest, and everything that gets through goes at them (`pickMark`, `heistAttack`). Spreading damage is only right on a poison plan (not this deck).
5. **Protect the engine before it goes down.** Boots/Greaves on Ramses first, then Etrata; Mithril Coat on Ramses; Fading Hope/Snapback to save Etrata (she returns without tax); don't deploy Ramses into open mana when he can wait a turn for protection, but his anthem is worth more than hiding him (measured: holding him cost 1.2 points, so he comes down and the counters protect him).
6. **Go for it when the window is there**, and aim to win second or third rather than first when a kill isn't on the table [S5, S11]: in bot games this means the attack only commits everything when the kill is real (`outcome().kill`), and otherwise sends only what gets through.

### Closing tools that fit this deck (what turns 15 damage a game into kills)
- **Ramses, Assassin Lord**: one opponent dying after an Assassin attacked them wins the game. He is the deck's win condition, the tutors' first target and the thing to protect.
- **Halvers + Bloodletter**: Quietus Spike, Scytheclaw, Virtus, Unstoppable Slasher, Shredder, Radioactive Man halve what's left; Bloodletter of Aclazotz doubles life loss on our turn, so one halver connecting takes everything. Wound Reflection and Archfiend of Despair are slower doublers.
- **One-shot halvers**: Blood Tribute (kicked by tapping Etrata, a Vampire), Rush of Dread; with Bloodletter either kills the target outright. **Hatred** turns any unblocked Assassin into a kill for X life.
- **Extra combat / double strike**: Genji Glove is the colorless repeatable extra combat [S30]; Leyline Axe and Fireshrieker give double strike (two cloaks per hit).
- **One-sided wipes**: Kindred Dominance (Assassin) and Massacre Wurm clear three boards and keep ours [S34, S38].
- **Team evasion**: Archetype of Imagination, Levitation, Eldrazi Monument, Cover of Darkness (fear is blocked by ~half the field's black/artifact creatures [S37]), Vela (intimidate on colorless face-downs: only artifact creatures block them), Dolmen Gate, Reverse the Polarity.
- **Overrun/anthems**: Coat of Arms (symmetric; cast only when our shared-type count beats theirs), Obelisk of Urd, Door of Destinies, Achilles, Ramses.
- **Theft is the card-advantage engine, not the finisher** [S62, S60]: every cloak is a body and, with Satoru/They Came from the Pipes/Kindred Discovery, a card.

### Deckbuilding numbers (from `../sources/bracket4-deckbuilding.md`)
- Bracket-4-labelled lists run **25–36 lands** (turbo 25–30, midrange 33–36), **2–11 fast-mana pieces**, **5–11 free or cheap counters**, **4–9 tutors**, **5–18 Game Changers**; a bracket calculator's heuristic is 10–18 interaction spells (15–28% of the deck), at least 40% of it at instant speed. cEDH, the ceiling, sits at 26–31 lands, 10–13 fast-mana artifacts, 23–35 instant-speed spells, average mana value 1.5–1.8 and two compact win conditions. No source gives a Bracket-4-specific average mana value; aggro guides say ≤2.8 (nerdleagues) or about 1.9 with 15+ instant-speed spells (airza).
- The five levers everyone names (Girten, EDHREC 2025-04-28): fast mana; the Game Changers that fit; free interaction; tutors plus redundant routes to the same win; draw engines. "Build for your best hand, not your average hand" (bluecore).
- What wins (Playgroup.gg, all brackets pooled, ~27,000 games a month in 2026): combat 55%, non-combat damage 21%, commander damage 9%, combo 6%, alternative wins 6%; decks with 4+ Game Changers win 11.9 points above baseline; games average 8.8 rounds, combo wins come at 7.0 rounds and combat wins at 9.2. Stated Bracket 4 win turns: 4–7.
- Dimir aggro's reputation: viable at Bracket 4 only as evasion-tempo with free counters, a cheap commander and an instant-speed backup (the Yuriko model), not as go-wide beatdown; Dimir has no Craterhoof, and its creature quality "falls off dramatically" past the cheap evasive bodies.

### Bracket 4 shell (from the EDHREC Bracket 4 Etrata data and the Yuriko template)
Fast mana (Sol Ring, Mana Vault, Chrome Mox, Lotus Petal, Dark Ritual, Cabal Ritual, Ancient Tomb, signets/talismans), the tutor suite (Demonic, Vampiric, Imperial Seal, Grim Tutor, Diabolic Intent, Demonic Consultation; Pyre of Heroes, Fleshwrither and the transmuters for Ramses), free interaction (Force of Will, Fierce Guardianship, Force of Negation, Flare of Denial, Deadly Rollick, Snuff Out, Flare of Malice, Mana Drain, Swan Song, An Offer You Can't Refuse), and draw engines (Rhystic Study, Mystic Remora, Dark Confidant, Esper Sentinel, Satoru, Kindred Discovery, Skullclamp). 30–33 lands.

## 5. Versions (`lists/`)
All three keep Etrata as the engine (Assassins connect, every hit cloaks) and Ramses as the kill, with Bloodletter and the halvers so that one connection on one player ends the game. They differ in what the rest of the 99 does.

- **v1-blitz** (31 lands, 8 fast-mana pieces, 8 tutors, 11 interaction spells of which 7 free or one-mana, 5 draw engines): the Yuriko template with Assassins. 27 cheap evasive bodies and lords, the kill kit (Ramses, Bloodletter, Quietus Spike, Virtus, Slasher, Shredder, Grievous Wound, Hatred), copies of Ramses (Spark Double, Sakashima), Leyline so stolen 2/2s steal again, protection (Boots, Greaves, Mithril Coat, Fading Hope) and Reanimate for Ramses. Rogue's Passage, Reverse the Polarity and Eldrazi Monument get the last attacker through.
- **v2-snowball** (32 lands): the Fugitives primer plan. Four type-changers (Leyline, Arcane Adaptation, Maskwood, Roshan) and Roaming Throne so every cloak is an Assassin that cloaks twice; three Etrata copies (Spark Double, Sakashima, Auton Soldier with myriad); team evasion (Vela's intimidate on colorless face-downs, Archetype of Imagination, Eldrazi Monument, Cover of Darkness); typal anthems (Coat of Arms, Obelisk of Urd, Achilles, Ramses); draw on every cloak (Satoru, They Came from the Pipes, Kindred Discovery); Thieving Amalgam and Orochi for more face-down bodies; Kindred Dominance as the one-sided wipe; Vein Ripper with Ashnod's Altar as the primer's non-combat closer. Fewer cheap Assassins, 6 tutors, 8 interaction.
- **v3-tempo** (30 lands, 14 interaction spells of which 10 free or cheap): the Yuriko list with Etrata's trigger. Nine ninjas (Ingenious Infiltrator, Ninja of the Deep Hours, Moon-Circuit Hacker, Prosperous Thief, Mistblade Shinobi, Thousand-Faced Shadow, Silver-Fur Master, Fallen Shinobi, Orochi) re-use eleven 0–2 mana evasive bodies; Leyline and Arcane Adaptation make them Assassins; the same kill kit and copies; Force of Despair, Flare of Malice, Flare of Denial, Mana Drain and the Forces as the free interaction.

Each is benched against both fields (2,016 games each, paired with skA2-0), then the best is hill-climbed by packages, confirmed with 5,040 games, and checked with NO_COMMANDER=1 and the telemetry.

## 6. What the first round of versions showed (2026-10-06)
Paired 2,016-game benches against the A2 skeleton (34.4% / 18.5%): v1 blitz 31.9% / 18.5%, v2 snowball 34.1% / 18.2%, v3 tempo 26.5% / 18.7%, v4 deathtouch-drain 27.7% / 18.4%. No shell beats the skeleton; the ninja shell is clearly worse against precons (ninjutsu bounces the cheap Assassins that are the steal engine) and the drain engine rarely assembles (6.4 life a game).

The diagnostics on v2 (`firstseen.js`, `castrate.js`, `trace.js`) say why every version lands in the same place:
- **Ramses is cast in 45% of games; the deck wins 52% of those and 19% of the rest.** Even perfect access to him caps the deck near 50%, so a second kill that doesn't need him is required, and his games have to convert more often.
- **In lost games the board never develops**: 3 creatures and 7 power from round 7 onward (5.7 creatures and 15 power in won games), while the hero loses 5–6 creatures a game on opponents' turns and faces 0.7 wipes. Hand size stays at 3.8 cards: the deck has cards but they're 5–7 mana closers it never reaches (Coat of Arms is cast in 5% of games, Obelisk 17%, Kindred Dominance and Vein Ripper rarely).
- **The cheap instant-speed kill is never used**: Hatred was cast in 2% of v1's games because the bot taps out in the first main phase; with Ramses out, Hatred on any unblocked Assassin is a kill from 40 life. Reverse the Polarity (54% wins when cast) and Eldrazi Monument (49%) are the cards that correlate most with winning when they land.
- Counters correlate with losing (Mana Drain 22% wins when cast, Force of Will 27%): they're cast from behind, mostly on opponents' commanders, and don't stop the wipes that decide the games.

Changes being measured from this: hold mana in main phase one when a Hatred or Blood Tribute kill is live; keep the last counter for a wipe or for removal on Ramses/Etrata; Training Grounds so the stolen cards get flipped; and a version built around the cards that convert a board (Eldrazi Monument, Reverse the Polarity, Coat of Arms) rather than around more 1/1s.

## 7. What real Etrata pilots say (from `../sources/etrata-strategy.md`, read 2026-10-06)
- **The plan that works at Bracket 3–4 is the cloak snowball**, not Assassin beatdown: one or two cheap evasive Assassins on turns 1–2, Etrata on turn 3 attacking at once (Lightning Greaves on turn 2 so she can), then copies or doublers of Etrata (the Sakashimas, Spark Double, Auton Soldier with myriad, Roaming Throne, Irenicus, Nanogene Conversion) and a type-changer (Maskwood, Arcane Adaptation, Conspiracy, Leyline, Roshan) so every stolen 2/2 cloaks again. Pilots report 64, 81 and 90 cloaks in a single attack once it goes off.
- **Ramses is a bonus** ("secret commander"), held until the weakest player can be killed and cast right before combat; the kill enablers they name are Unstoppable Slasher, Grievous Wound and Virtus with evasion, one-sided wipes (Kindred Dominance, Raise the Palisade) and Cyclonic Rift to open a board. Bloodletter, Blood Tribute and Rush of Dread appear in no Etrata source.
- **Finishers they trust:** an evasive swarm of 2/2s (Cover of Darkness is the most cited enabler, then Wonder, Levitation, Dolmen Gate, Haunted One, Coat of Arms), decking the player you keep hitting, and altar + Vein Ripper or Zulaport drain; Meathook Massacre and Strixhaven Stadium as alternates. Called too slow: Thieving Amalgam, Tasha, Titan of Littjara, Agent of Treachery, the Silencer hit-counter line, four-mana Assassins.
- **Flipping is a sideline** above Bracket 2: about one activation a turn, for bombs or stolen counterspells; Training Grounds is what makes it real; stolen lands are flipped as ramp; duds become sacrifice fodder.
- **Piloting rules:** keep two or more lands plus a 1–2 mana Assassin with Etrata on curve; cast Etrata only when an Assassin connects that turn and with protection in hand (she is "kill on sight"); hit the open player, but choose by deck; **save counters for the wipe and let spot removal through** ("letting them kill single plays is superior to being shields-down"); play stolen cards before your own; and don't focus one opponent to death, because their cards leave with them.
- **Why they lose:** Etrata dies, wraths ("absolutely folds to board wipes"), the four-mana flip tax, random hits, two half-plans, 2/2s that don't close. Fixes: counters and recursion, clones (the single most repeated recommendation), type-changer redundancy or tutors, 37–39 lands in budget builds, spread attacks.
- **Bracket 4:** the two Bracket 4 primers build with fast mana, every black tutor, free counters (Fierce Guardianship in 67% of EDHREC's Bracket 4 Etrata decks), Rhystic Study/Mystic Remora, and a non-combat ending (altar + Vein Ripper, Thassa's Oracle, Bolas's Citadel). r/CompetitiveEDH's view is that Etrata "cannot hang" above Bracket 3 as aggro; honest self-placements are "high Bracket 3 at best". Logged results: 9–4 over thirteen Bracket 2–3 games (one pilot), 7–20 at Bracket 3 (another). No Bracket 4 win log exists.

**What this changes in the engine work:** the counter policy (save the last counter for the wipe) measured +1.0 and is now the default; Teferi's Veil (attackers phase out through opponents' turns) and Greaves on Etrata first are being measured; Training Grounds is in. The pilots' main lever, the exponential cloak snowball, needs the board to survive long enough to double, which is exactly what the bot games show it doesn't do against precon wraths.

## 8. The kill kit doesn't convert; Ramses access does (v2, 2,016 games vs precons)
Win rate by which pieces had entered the battlefield: Ramses without Bloodletter 51% (627 games), Ramses + Bloodletter 56% (438), Ramses + a halver 50% (423), Ramses + Bloodletter + a halver 51% (178); no Ramses: Bloodletter 26%, Bloodletter + halver 36%, neither 10% (665). By round 7: Ramses alone 52%, Ramses + Bloodletter 60% (149). Ramses + a type-changer 55%.

So once Ramses is out the deck wins about half its games whatever else it holds, and the halver/doubler package adds little on top of him (it does help without him: 10% → 26–36%). The deck's win rate is, to a first approximation, 0.52 × P(Ramses lands) + 0.19 × P(he doesn't). Ramses lands in 52% of games. **Getting him out in every game, early, and keeping him there is the whole problem**: tutor density and card flow, copies, recursion, protection, and surviving long enough to cast him (he leaves the battlefield in 36% of the games he reaches). The 5–7 mana closers v2 carries (Orochi, Vela, Kindred Dominance, Archetype, Vein Ripper, Thieving Amalgam, Auton Soldier) are cast in 5–15% of games and are the slots to spend on that.

## 9. Ramses that stays wins (v2b, 2,016 games vs precons)
Of the 505 games where Ramses entered by round 6, only 59 were lost with him still on the battlefield: the deck wins about 80% of the games in which he lands early and survives, and most of its losses with him are the games where he is removed (he leaves in 36% of the games he reaches, almost always to removal or a wrath on an opponent's turn). More tutors raised the share of games with Ramses from 52% to 61% without raising the win rate (experiment 41), so access is no longer the bottleneck; **keeping him on the battlefield is**. The levers: hexproof or shroud equipment (Lightning Greaves, Swiftfoot Boots, Whispersilk Cloak), indestructible against destroy effects (Darksteel Plate), the counter saved for removal on him (default now), recursion (Reanimate, Patriarch's Bidding), copies (Spark Double, Sakashima), and a tutor rule that fetches protection the moment he is out and bare.

## 10. Where the ceiling is, and why (2026-10-06, after 47 experiments)
Three numbers describe every version of this deck in the bot games:
- **With Ramses on the battlefield the deck wins about half its games; without him, a tenth to a fifth.** That has held for every list from the first skeleton to v2c (51% / 10–19%). The win rate is therefore about 0.5 × P(Ramses lands and stays) + 0.15 × P(he doesn't).
- **He lands in 52–62% of games and is removed in about a third of those**, mostly by the precons' wraths on their own turns (Cleansing Nova, Time Wipe, Phyrexian Rebirth, Wrath of God, Austere Command, Hour of Reckoning). When he lands by round 6 and stays, the deck wins about 80%.
- **Everything else is a point.** Each piloting rule from the research (mulligan, counter held for the wipe, Etrata when an Assassin connects, Teferi's Veil, the low curve) added 0.2–2 points; no card other than Ramses, Bloodletter, Sol Ring and the three cheapest tutors measured outside ±1.5 of a basic land.

The levers tried on the three numbers and what they gave:
| Lever | What was tried | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P(lands) | +4 Ramses tutors (#41) | presence 52% → 61%, wins unchanged: the extra tutors are dead once he's out |
| P(lands) | Pyre of Heroes, Fleshwrither, Dimir House Guard, Demonic Consultation (#ra-*) | +0.6 to +2.3 vs precons; Pyre and Consultation kept |
| P(stays) | Greaves/Boots first, Ramses held until protected (#47) | −3.8 / −4.8: a turn of tempo costs more than the removal saves |
| P(stays) | Whispersilk Cloak, Darksteel Plate, Patriarch's Bidding (#44, #45) | −2.3 / +0.6; tutoring for them: nothing |
| P(stays) | the last counter saved for the wipe (#33) | +1.0 |
| P(stays) | Teferi's Veil (#36), Eldrazi Monument (sweep 2) | +1.0, +1.9 |
| win without him | kill kit (halvers + Bloodletter) tutored (#12, pk-kit) | −4.7 / −8.5: the kit doesn't convert without the "you win" |
| win without him | the vampire loop (#23, v2c-vamp) | +3.9 vs precons: the one package that lifts the no-Ramses games, and it's a non-combat closer |
| speed | the low curve (#42) | +2.1 vs Bracket 4 |

So the honest ceiling of a combat-only Etrata deck in this engine is about 37% against the precon bots and about 21% against the Bracket 4 bots, with the average winning round at 8.8 / 7.8, and the one thing that moves it further is a non-combat closer. The reasons are structural rather than card choices: the deck's creatures are 1/1s and 2/2s facing three opponents' blockers and wraths, its kill depends on one 4-mana legendary creature surviving a turn cycle, and the stolen cards are 2/2s until mana is spent on them. The pilots' placement of aggro Etrata ("high Bracket 3 at best") matches.

## 11. The last hill-climb (2026-10-06, 11:00): what the cut sweep of the finalist said
- Six cards were worth less than a basic land on both fields (Kindred Discovery, Swiftfoot Boots, Mana Drain, Interceptor, Obelisk of Urd, Ghostly Flicker); replacing all six with basics is the largest single gain of the project: +4.4 / +2.6 confirmed (40.4% / 23.6%). The mana is then saturated: 42 lands measured +0.2 / −0.2.
- The freed slots are not worth spending on ideas: recursion (Patriarch's Bidding, Kindred Dominance, Shredder), Etrata copies (Auton Soldier, Thieving Amalgam, Vela), evasion (Levitation, Archetype of Imagination, Training Grounds) and one-shot reach (Hatred, Blood Tribute, Exsanguinate, Rush of Dread) all measured below the basics in those slots, and within ±2 of them once the deck already had 38 lands.
- Ramses dependence is a constant of the archetype, not of the list: every version wins 50–57% with him and 14–21% without, and the only package that lifts the games without him is the vampire loop (38% without him in the closer list). The levers that work act on how often he lands and stays (mana, tutors, Teferi's Veil, the saved counter), not on winning without him.
- The sweep's single-card numbers are additive here but regress as a package (+11.3 summed at 2,016 games → +7.0 at 5,040): confirm packages, don't trust the sweep's best rows at face value.
