# Arcs (base game): rules summary for a beginner tutorial

Written in my own words from the official Leder Games rulebook (`arcs-base-rulebook-leder.pdf`, 24 pages, the build dated August 27, 2025). Page numbers (p.) are that rulebook's own. Base game only. This summary leaves out the Blighted Reach campaign and only touches on Leaders & Lore.

**Confidence markers**
- No marker: stated in the rulebook text.
- **[from components]**: the PDF text extraction lost an icon or a number, so I read it from the official dev-kit images (`arcs-raw/icon and punchboard/`, `arcs-raw/devkit/`) or from photos of the real components (`arcs-raw/images/`). I'm confident about it, but it isn't a rulebook sentence.
- **[verify]**: I couldn't pin it down for sure. Check it against a card or board image before using it as a quiz answer.

## 0. The game in one paragraph (p.2, p.6)

Each player leads a spacefaring society in the Reach. You score **Power**, which works like victory points. The game ends at the end of a chapter if someone has **27 Power (4 players)**, **30 (3 players)** or **33 (2 players)**, or after **chapter 5**. The player with the most Power wins. You only gain Power by being best at **ambitions**, objectives that get "declared" during play and are scored at the end of each chapter. A game usually lasts **3 to 5 chapters**. Expect about 45 minutes per player for a first game and about 30 once you know it.

## 1. Components (p.2–3, p.6)

### Per player (the box has 4 sets)
| Piece | Count | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Player board | 1 | Holds resources, your unbuilt cities, Trophies and Captives |
| Ships | **15** | Move on the map and fight |
| Cities | **5** | Built on planets. You tax them for resources. They start on the player board. |
| Starports | **5** | Built on planets. They build ships and allow Catapult moves. |
| Agents | **10** | Placed on Court cards (influence). Rivals can capture them. |
| Power markers | 2 | One tracks your Power. The second is only used in expansions. |

### Shared
- **Map**: 6 clusters, plus the Power track along the bottom edge, the Chapter track, the ambition boxes on the right edge with an "Available Markers" area, and the Lead and "Surpass, Copy, or Pivot" card slots on the left edge.
- **28 action cards**: 4 suits × numbers 1–7. The "1" and "7" cards (8 cards) are used **only with 4 players**.
- **25 Guild cards and 6 Vox cards**, which together make the Court deck (31 cards).
- **12 setup cards**: 4 each for 2, 3 and 4 players.
- 8 Leader and 14 Lore cards, used only in Leaders & Lore mode.
- **25 resource tokens**: 5 each of Material, Fuel, Weapons, Relics, Psionic.
- **18 battle dice**: 6 Assault, 6 Skirmish, 6 Raid.
- **3 ambition markers**, plus the **zero marker**, the **initiative marker** and the **chapter marker**.
- 4 path markers (2 long, 2 short), 6 out-of-play markers, 4 aid sheets.

### The 5 resources
These match the 5 planet types: **Material, Fuel, Weapons, Relics, Psionic** (p.7, p.17).

### Battle dice faces [from components]
The dev-kit has one image per face. File names show which faces repeat.

**Icon key**
- **Hit** (star burst): damages one defending ship. If no defending ships are left, it hits a building instead.
- **Self-hit** (flame): damages one of your own attacking ships.
- **Intercept** (crosshair ring): if you rolled any, the defender hits your attacking ships once per fresh defending ship. This happens at most once per battle.
- **Building hit** (burst with a triangle inside): hits a defending building directly.
- **Key**: spent to raid resources and Guild cards.

| Die | Faces |
|---|---|
| **Assault** (red) | 1: hit + intercept · 2: hit + self-hit · 3: 2 hits + self-hit · 4: 2 hits · 5: hit + self-hit · 6: blank |
| **Skirmish** (blue) | 3 faces: 1 hit · the other faces: blank. Files are named "1-3" (hit) and "4-5" (blank); I'm assuming the sixth face is blank too **[verify]**. |
| **Raid** (orange) | 1: 2 keys + intercept · 2: key + self-hit · 3 and 4: building hit + self-hit · 5: building hit + key · 6: intercept only |

How the rulebook describes them (p.14): Assault damages fast but costs you. Skirmish damages slowly but is safe. Raid steals things and can damage buildings, but it's risky.

### Board layout (p.6)
- **6 clusters**. Each has **4 systems**: **3 planets and 1 gate**. The gate shows the cluster number (1–6).
- **Adjacency** means sharing a thin border:
  - A gate is adjacent to the 3 planets of its own cluster and to the gates of the 2 neighbouring clusters.
  - A planet is adjacent to its own cluster's gate and to one or both neighbouring planets. Planets separated by a thick, irregular border are **not** adjacent.
- The centre of the map (the Arcs logo) is not in play.
- Each planet has a **planet type** (one of the 5 resources) and **1 or 2 building slots** for cities or starports. It also shows an ID symbol (arrow, hex or moon), which only expansions use.
- **Out-of-play clusters**: the setup card marks **2 clusters with 2–3 players** and **1 cluster with 4 players** (p.4, step J). For each one, put a path marker on its gate and an out-of-play marker on each of the **3 planets** that touch that gate. The cluster is out of play in every way. The path marker connects the gates of the two neighbouring clusters, so you can move between them in one move.

## 2. Setup (p.4–5)

### Table (steps A–K)
- **A.** Lay out the map. Put the 18 dice and 25 resource tokens nearby as the supply.
- **B.** Give the **initiative marker** to a random player.
- **C.** Shuffle the **20 action cards numbered 2–6** to form the action deck.
- **D.** **4 players only:** shuffle in the 8 cards numbered "1" and "7". With 2–3 players these go back in the box, so the deck has **28 cards with 4 players and 20 with 2–3 players**.
- **E.** Put the 3 ambition markers **blue side up** (the lower-value side) in "Available Markers".
- **F.** Put the chapter marker on space "1".
- **G.** Put the zero marker in the "Ambition Declared" slot on the map.
- **H.** Shuffle the **6 Vox and 25 Guild cards** into the Court deck. Deal a face-up Court row of **3 cards (2 players)** or **4 cards (3–4 players)**.
- **I.** Shuffle the setup cards for your player count and draw **1**.
- **J.** Cover the out-of-play cluster(s) that card shows: a path marker on the gate and 3 out-of-play markers on the planets.
- **K.** **2 players only:** take the 6 resource tokens matching the 6 covered planets and put them on the ambition boxes:
  - Material and Fuel go on **Tycoon**
  - Weapons go on **Warlord**
  - Relics go on **Keeper**
  - Psionics go on **Empath**

### Players (steps L–Q)
- **L.** Each player takes a colour and its player board, 5 cities, 5 starports, 15 ships and 10 agents. Put the 5 cities in the **triangle slots along the top of your player board**.
- **M.** Put your Power marker on **0**.
- **N.** Place starting pieces at the same time. The player with initiative is player "1" and uses the systems marked 1A, 1B and 1C on the setup card:
  - **1A**: 3 ships + 1 city (take the **leftmost** city from your board)
  - **1B**: 3 ships + 1 starport
  - **1C**: 2 ships. With 2 players there are **two** 1C systems, and each gets 2 ships.
  - Going clockwise, the 2nd, 3rd and 4th players do the same in 2A–C, 3A–C and 4A–C.
- **O.** Each player gains **2 resources**, one matching the planet type of their A system and one matching their B system. They go in the leftmost and second-leftmost circular resource slots of the player board.
- **P.** Everyone draws **6 action cards** (the hand size). **2 players only:** the player without initiative may discard all 6 and draw 6 new ones, once (a mulligan).
- **Q.** Put the rest of the action deck face down as the action discard pile on the map and shuffle it.

**Starting pieces on the map:** 8 ships, 1 city and 1 starport per player (with 2 players, 10 ships, because there are two 1C systems).

## 3. Playing a chapter (p.8–11)

A **chapter** is a run of **rounds** that continues until nobody has action cards left. In a **round**, each player takes **one turn**, starting with the player who has initiative and going clockwise.

### Step 1: the initiative player leads (p.8–9)
- They must play one action card **face up** as the **lead card** and put it in the "Lead" slot on the map.
- They may take **one action per action pip** on the lead card, choosing from the actions its suit allows.
- They may **declare an ambition** first (see §4).
- **Passing:** if they have no cards, or choose not to play one, they must pass the initiative. Give the marker to the next player clockwise **who still has cards**, and the round **ends at once**. That player leads the new round.
- If everyone with cards passes one after another, discard all action cards and the chapter ends.

### Step 2: everyone else, in clockwise order (p.10)
Players with no cards skip their turn. Each other player must play one card in **one of three ways**:

| Option | How you play the card | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Surpass** | Face up, **same suit as the lead card** and a **higher number than the lead card** | Actions **up to the number of pips** on your card |
| **Copy** | **Any** card, **face down** | **One** action from the **lead card's** suit |
| **Pivot** | Face up, a card of a **different suit** from the lead (any number) | **One** action from **your own card's** suit |

- You only need to beat the **lead card**, not every card played so far.
- Pivoting does **not** change the lead suit.
- When the leader declared an ambition, the lead card counts as **0** (the zero marker covers its number), so any card of that suit Surpasses it.

### Seizing the initiative (p.10)
On your turn, after you play your card and before any action, you may seize the initiative if **you don't already hold it** and **nobody has seized it yet this round**. There are two ways:
1. Play **one extra action card face down** beside your card. It gives no actions.
2. **Surpass with a "7"** card. You still get its actions. This only exists with 4 players.

Take the initiative marker and **lay it on its side** to show it's been seized. No one else can seize it this round. If it has already been seized, you can still Surpass with a 7; you just don't seize.

### Step 3: who gets initiative next (p.11)
- If someone **seized** it, they keep it.
- If not, it goes to the player who **Surpassed with the highest card number**.
- If nobody Surpassed, it **stays** with the current holder.

### Step 4: clean up (p.11)
Discard every played card, including cards used to seize, **face down** into the action discard pile. If anyone still has cards, start a new round. Otherwise the chapter ends.

**Note:** players can run out of cards at different times, because seizing uses up an extra card. (Variant on p.22: split the discard into face-up and face-down piles for a more strategic game.)

## 4. Action cards and declaring ambitions (p.8–9, p.18)

### Suits and the actions they allow (p.8)
| Suit | Actions |
|---|---|
| **Administration** | Tax, Repair, Influence |
| **Aggression** | Battle, Move, Secure |
| **Construction** | Build, Repair |
| **Mobilization** | Move, Influence |

### Numbers and pips
- The numbers run from 1 to 7: 2–6 in every game, and 1 and 7 with 4 players only.
- **Pips** are the action count, shown in the top-left under the number.
- Examples from the rulebook and photos: Construction **4** = 3 pips (p.8), Construction **5** = 2 actions (p.10), Administration **4** = 3 pips, Construction **6** = 2 pips, Mobilization **2** = 4 pips (photo bgg-19).
- From those I infer: **1–2 = 4 pips, 3–4 = 3 pips, 5–6 = 2 pips, 7 = 1 pip** **[verify the 1, 3 and 7 cards]**. The trade-off: a high number wins initiative but gives fewer actions.

### Declaring an ambition (p.9)
- **Who:** only the player with initiative, on the **lead card**, before taking any action (and before any Prelude action, p.20).
- **Which ambition:**
  - "1" cards have no ambition.
  - "2" to "6" cards show one ambition (an icon in the bottom-left corner). The order follows the ambition list on p.18: **2 = Tycoon, 3 = Tyrant, 4 = Warlord, 5 = Keeper, 6 = Empath**. The p.9 example confirms that a "4" declares Warlord. **[from components; double-check 2/3/5/6 on a card image]**
  - "7" cards let you declare **any** ambition.
- **How:**
  1. Take the **highest-value** marker from Available Markers and put it in that ambition's box.
  2. Put the **zero marker** on the lead card's number, so the card's number is now **0**. Its pips are **not** covered, so you keep all your actions.
- **Limits:**
  - Only one declaration per lead card (you can't declare twice with the same card), so at most one per round.
  - You can't declare if all 3 markers are already placed.
  - You **may** declare an ambition that's already declared; a second marker goes in its box.
- **Why it costs something:** the lead card counts as 0, so anyone holding a card of that suit can Surpass it and take initiative.

### Ambition marker values [from components: dev-kit file names `punchboard ambition front 2-0 / 3-2 / 5-3` and `back 4-2 / 6-3 / 9-4`]
| Marker | Blue side (start): 1st / 2nd | Flipped side: 1st / 2nd |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 2 / 0 | 4 / 2 |
| Middle | 3 / 2 | 6 / 3 |
| High | 5 / 3 | 9 / 4 |

- The rulebook's Tycoon example (p.18) adds up "7 Power" with a tie paying 3 each. That matches the blue 5/3 marker plus the blue 2/0 marker in the same box: 5 + 2 = 7 for first, 3 + 0 = 3 for second.
- **Flipping (p.19):** at the end of each chapter, flip the **lowest-value marker that hasn't been flipped yet** to its higher side. Ambitions are worth more as the game goes on.

## 5. Standard actions (p.12–16)

**Words to know first** (p.7, p.12)
- **Loyal**: your colour. **Rival**: another player's colour.
- **Fresh**: an upright ship, or a building on its lighter side. **Damaged**: a ship tipped over, or a building flipped to its darker side. A damaged piece that is hit again is **destroyed** (removed).
- **Control** (p.7): you control a system and everything in it if you have **more fresh ships there than each Rival**. On a tie, nobody controls it. Control lets you:
  - tax Rival cities there (and capture their agents)
  - stop Rival ships from passing through a gate on a Catapult move
  - make Rivals' new pieces there arrive damaged

### Tax (Administration) (p.12)
- Choose a **Loyal city**, or a **Rival city in a system you control**. Gain 1 resource of that planet's type from the supply.
- If it's a Rival city, you also **capture 1 of that Rival's agents** from their supply into your Captives box, even if you couldn't gain the resource.
- **Limit:** each city can be taxed **only once per turn**.

### Build (Construction) (p.12)
- **A building:** place 1 starport or city in an **empty building slot** in a system where you have a **Loyal piece**. A city always comes from the **leftmost** city still on your player board.
- **A ship:** place 1 ship at a **Loyal starport**. Each starport builds **at most one ship per turn**.
- If **anyone else controls** that system, the new piece arrives **damaged**.
- Building costs no resources; it just uses an action.

### Move (Mobilization, Aggression) (p.13)
- Move **any number** of Loyal ships from **one system to one adjacent system**.
- **Catapult:** if the ships start in a system with a **Loyal starport**, they can keep moving as far as you like, dropping ships off along the way. They must stop when they enter a **gate controlled by someone else** or **any planet**.
  - Control is checked **before** entering.
  - A Rival starport you happen to control doesn't let you Catapult.
- You can't enter out-of-play systems. The path marker makes the two neighbouring gates adjacent.
- There is no fuel cost. Fuel only gives an **extra** Move (see §6).

### Repair (Construction, Administration) (p.13)
Stand up 1 damaged Loyal ship, or flip 1 damaged Loyal building back to fresh. It can be **anywhere** on the map.

### Influence (Mobilization, Administration) (p.13)
Place 1 of your agents on **any** card in the Court.

### Secure (Aggression) (p.13)
- Take 1 Court card if you have **more Loyal agents on it than each Rival**.
- Your agents on it go back to your supply. **All Rival agents on it become your Captives.**
- Resolve its "When Secured" effect, if it has one. If it tells you to discard the card, put it in a face-up Court discard pile.
- Refill that Court spot from the Court deck.
- You can't use the secured card's Prelude actions in the same Prelude you secured it.

### Battle (Aggression) (p.14–16)
1. **Battle system:** choose 1 system that has Loyal ships. Those ships are the attackers.
2. **Defender:** choose **one** Rival with pieces there.
3. **Dice:** take up to **1 die per attacking ship**, of any types and mixed as you like. You can take fewer, but never more than **6 of one type**.
   - **Raid dice limit:** you can only take Raid dice if the defender has **buildings in this system**, or has **no Loyal buildings anywhere** on the map.
4. **Roll, then resolve in this exact order.** The attacker resolves everything; the defender usually makes no choices.
   1. **Self-hits:** hit your own attacking ships, once per self-hit icon.
   2. **Intercept:** if **any** intercept icon came up, the defender hits your attacking ships **once per fresh defending ship**. This happens once per battle, however many intercepts you rolled.
   3. **Hits:** hit defending ships, one per hit icon. Once no defending ships are left, the rest hit defending buildings.
   4. **Building hits:** hit defending buildings, one per icon.
   5. **Raid:** if you still have attacking ships, spend **keys** to steal.
- **Damage:**
  - Hitting a fresh piece **damages** it (tip the ship, flip the building).
  - Hitting a damaged piece **destroys** it.
  - The attacker chooses which of their own ships to hit. Example p.15: Mikah takes 2 hits on two different fresh ships rather than destroying one.
- **Trophies:**
  - Defending pieces the attacker destroys go to the **attacker's Trophies**.
  - Attacking pieces destroyed by self-hits or intercept go to the **defender's Trophies**.
- **Raiding (p.16):** spend keys, one item at a time. To steal a resource from the defender's player board, pay the **raid cost printed above its slot**. To steal a Guild card, pay the raid cost in its **top-left corner**.

### Destroying a city: Outrage and Ransacking (p.16)
This applies whenever you destroy a city, **even outside battle**.
1. **Provoke Outrage:**
   - Discard all your resources **and** Guild cards of that city's planet type. The cards go to the Court discard pile.
   - Put one of your agents on that resource's **Outrage icon** on your player board.
   - From now on you **can't spend** that resource for its Prelude action. You can still tax it, and it still counts for ambitions.
   - If you're already Outraged for that type, discard again but don't place another agent.
   - If you have no agents in your supply, use a placeholder until one comes back.
2. **Ransack the Court:** secure **one** Court card that has **any** of the defender's agents on it. **All Rival agents on it become your Trophies, not Captives.** Refill the Court.

### Ruling / controlling: what it gives
The rulebook uses only "control"; there is no separate "rule" term in the base game. See the Control bullets at the top of this section. There's no Power for holding territory. Territory matters because it lets you tax, build safely, and feeds ambitions.

## 6. Resources (p.17)

### Where they come from
- Taxing cities (p.12)
- Raiding in battle (p.16)
- Some cards
- 2 at setup

### Slots
- You hold resource tokens in the **circular resource slots** on your player board. Building cities **opens more slots**.
- Each slot has a **raid cost** printed above it, from 1 to 3 keys. In the photos the leftmost slot costs 3 keys and the next two cost 1 each **[from components; exact sequence for every slot: verify on bgg-16 / bgg-17]**.
- **No space:** when you gain a resource, you may rearrange your tokens, but anything you can't hold is **discarded**. The same happens when a city comes back to your board and covers a slot.

### Spending resources: Prelude actions (p.17, p.20)
Your **Prelude** starts when you play your action card and ends the moment you spend one of its pips. During it you may spend any number and mix of resources:

| Resource | Prelude action |
|---|---|
| **Material** | One Build or Repair |
| **Fuel** | One Move |
| **Weapon** | Your card's pips may also be used for **Battle** this turn, as if the card said "or Battle". It doesn't turn other resources' actions into Battle. |
| **Relic** | One Secure |
| **Psionic** | One action listed on the **lead card** |

**Limits**
- Spent resources return to the supply at the **end** of the Prelude, so you can't regain the same token during that Prelude.
- You can't spend resources if you pass the initiative or have no cards.
- You can't spend **Outraged** resources this way.
- You must decide whether to **declare an ambition** or **seize the initiative** **before** any Prelude action.

**Cards that hold resources:** those resources count for ambitions and can be spent, but they can't be stolen unless the card says so. They stay on the card if the card is stolen, and return to the supply if the card is discarded, buried or scrapped.

## 7. Cities on the player board (p.7, p.12, p.18–19)

- Your 5 cities start in the **triangle slots along the top** of your player board. One goes onto the map at setup (the leftmost).
- Each city you build comes from the **leftmost** remaining slot. Building it **uncovers**:
  - new **resource slots**
  - **Power bonuses**: the "+2 to won ambitions" and "+3 to won ambitions" slots
- **Bonus city Power (p.18):** each time you win **outright first place** in an ambition (not tied), gain **+2** if the "+2" slot is uncovered, or **+5** if both "+2" and "+3" are uncovered. You get this **once per ambition**, however many markers are in its box. Which city positions hide these bonuses is printed on the board **[verify on a player-board image]**.
- **Losing a city:** a destroyed city goes to the destroyer's Trophies (p.15). Any returned city goes to the **rightmost empty** city slot on its owner's board (p.19, p.22), which can cover resource slots or bonuses again.

## 8. The Court: Guild and Vox cards (p.7, p.13, p.17, p.20)

- **The Court** is the face-up row of 3 (2 players) or 4 (3–4 players) cards beside the map, dealt from the 31-card deck (25 Guild + 6 Vox).
- **Getting a card:**
  1. **Influence** puts your agents on it.
  2. **Secure** takes it if you have strictly more agents on it than each Rival. Relic spent in Prelude, or Aggression pips, give you Secure.
- **Agents when a card is secured:** the securer's own agents go back to their supply. Everyone else's agents become the securer's **Captives**, which count for **Tyrant**. When a Court card is taken by **Ransacking**, the Rival agents become **Trophies** instead.
- **Guild cards (25)** have three parts:
  - **Suit icon:** one of the 5 resources. It counts for ambitions like a token: Material and Fuel count for Tycoon, Relic for Keeper, Psionic for Empath. **Weapon cards count for no ambition.**
  - **Rules:** new actions or modifiers.
  - **Raid cost** (top-left): the keys an attacker needs to steal it.
  - You keep Guild cards in your play area. Rivals can **steal** them in battle, and you lose them through Outrage.
- **Vox cards (6)** represent incidents. You gain them from the Court and they **resolve immediately**.
- **Card rule types (p.20):**
  - **"Prelude:"** an extra action during your Prelude. Some of these discard the card.
  - **New actions** written like "Assassinate (Battle)": whenever you'd take the named standard action (with a pip or a resource), you may take the new action instead.
  - **Modifiers** (shown in bold): change standard actions, and sometimes setup, scoring, declaring or seizing. Modifiers don't affect new actions, but they do affect standard actions inside a new action.

## 9. Ending a chapter (p.18–19)

**When:** everyone is out of action cards, or everyone with cards passes the initiative in a row.

### Step 1: score every ambition that has at least one marker in its box
| Ambition | Card number | Who wins it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tycoon** | 2 | Most **Fuel + Material** icons, from resource tokens and Guild cards together |
| **Tyrant** | 3 | Most **Captives** (agents captured by taxing Rival cities and by securing) |
| **Warlord** | 4 | Most **Trophies** (pieces destroyed, mainly in battle) |
| **Keeper** | 5 | Most **Relic** icons (tokens + Guild cards) |
| **Empath** | 6 | Most **Psionic** icons (tokens + Guild cards) |

- **First place** gets the **sum of the higher numbers** on every marker in that box. **Second place** gets the sum of the lower numbers.
- **Anyone can score.** Who declared the ambition doesn't matter.
- **Ties:**
  - A tie for first: all tied players get **second-place** Power, and nobody else scores.
  - A tie for second: those players get nothing.
- **Qualifying:** you need **at least one** of the counted thing to score. For example, if no one has Trophies, Warlord pays nobody.
- **Bonus city Power:** +2 or +5 for outright first place only (see §7).
- **Two players (p.19):** the 6 tokens put on the ambition boxes at setup count as a **third, phantom player** for scoring. Weapons on Warlord count as Trophies. They can take first or second place and push a real player down.
- **Cards can change scoring** (p.20, modifiers). Two examples printed on p.3:
  - A "Just" lore effect changes Tyrant: no Power for second place, and you score the second-place value when you're first.
  - A Guild card in the April 2024 build: "After scoring, Rivals discard all Material."

### Step 2: clean up and flip (p.19)
- If **Warlord** was scored, **everyone returns all Trophies**. If **Tyrant** was scored, **everyone returns all Captives**.
- Returned pieces go back to their owners. Ships, starports and agents go to their supplies. Cities go to the rightmost empty city slot on their owner's board.
- If Warlord or Tyrant wasn't scored, Trophies and Captives **carry over** to the next chapter. (This is implied: only the scored one is returned.)
- Return all ambition markers to Available Markers. Flip the **lowest-value marker not yet flipped** to its higher side.

### Step 3: game end?
- The game ends if anyone has **at least 27 Power (4 players), 30 (3 players) or 33 (2 players)**, **or** the chapter marker is on **Chapter 5**.
- The most Power wins. **Tiebreak:** the tied player **earliest in turn order** wins (turn order starts with the initiative holder and goes clockwise).
- Otherwise, move the chapter marker forward.

### Step 4: new hands
- Shuffle **all** action cards and deal **6 each**. The rest become the face-down discard pile, shuffled.
- The **initiative holder leads** the next chapter.
- **2 players:** the player without initiative may mulligan once.

## 10. Fine print and common confusions (p.20–22)

**Fine print**
- **Rules hierarchy:**
  - The rulebook beats the aid sheets and player boards. Cards beat the rulebook.
  - A rule that says **"cannot"** is absolute unless a card explicitly says **"ignore"**.
  - Sidebars and italic text in parentheses are only reminders.
- **Ties and choice order:** break unspecified ties in turn order. Things that happen at the same time are ordered by the player taking the turn, or by the initiative holder if nobody is.
- **Elimination:** if you have no ships and no starports on the map, place **3 fresh ships in any gate** at the end of your turn. Conceding is allowed, usually in 2-player games.
- **Piece limits:**
  - You're limited to what's in the box. The exception is agents for Outrage, where you use a placeholder.
  - If you must place or return more than you can, do as much as possible.
  - "Place" means fresh Loyal pieces unless the rule says otherwise.
  - **Replacing** a piece isn't destroying it; the new piece keeps the old one's damaged state.
- **Negotiation:** deals aren't binding, and you can't give pieces unless a rule allows it.
- **Hidden hands:** you can't show your action cards to anyone.
- **Etiquette:** declaring and seizing technically come first, but people usually let you do them later in your turn, as long as nothing new has been revealed (no dice rolled, no card drawn).

**Common beginner confusions**
1. You only need to Surpass the **lead** card, not the highest card played.
2. Copy uses the **lead** card's actions; Pivot uses **your** card's actions. Both give only 1 action.
3. Declaring sets the lead card to 0 but **keeps its pips**.
4. Initiative goes to the **highest Surpass**, not the highest card overall. Pivots and Copies never win initiative.
5. Control counts **fresh ships only**. Damaged ships and buildings don't count.
6. Building in a system someone else controls gives you **damaged** pieces.
7. Catapulting needs a **Loyal starport** where the ships start, and stops on any planet or at a gate someone else controls.
8. Intercept happens **once** per battle, however many intercept faces you rolled.
9. Hits must go to ships before buildings, except building-hit icons, which go straight to buildings.
10. Raid dice need defending buildings in the battle system, or a defender with no buildings at all.
11. Destroying a city **hurts you too** (Outrage), but lets you Ransack the Court.
12. A tie for first place pays **second-place** Power, and no city bonus.
13. You need at least one of the counted thing to score an ambition.
14. Trophies and Captives reset only when Warlord or Tyrant is actually scored.
15. Weapon resources and Weapon Guild cards count for **no** ambition (except the 2-player phantom Weapons on Warlord).

### Glossary (p.23), one line each, in my words
- **Adjacent**: shares a thin border on the map.
- **Building**: a city or a starport.
- **Bury**: put on the bottom of the Court deck.
- **Capture**: put the piece in your Captives box.
- **Chapter**: the rounds played until everyone is out of action cards.
- **Clear**: return every piece from the named area.
- **Control**: more fresh ships in a system than each Rival. A tie means nobody controls it.
- **Damaged**: a tipped ship or a flipped building. Not fresh.
- **Destroy**: remove a piece from the map, often into a Trophies box.
- **Empty**: holds no pieces.
- **Fresh**: an upright ship or a building on its light side.
- **Gain**: take from the supply into your play area (impossible if the supply is empty).
- **Give**: put a piece into a Rival's play area.
- **Hit**: damage a fresh piece, or remove a damaged one.
- **Loyal**: your colour.
- **Out of Play**: can't be interacted with.
- **Piece**: any game component.
- **Place**: put a fresh Loyal piece in (see Pieces & Piece Limits, p.22).
- **Play Area**: your player board and the space around it.
- **Prelude**: the window after playing your card and before spending its first pip.
- **Replace / Return**: swap a piece for another while keeping its damage state / send a piece back to where it came from.
- **Rival**: another player's colour, or another player.
- **Round**: each player takes one turn.
- **Scrap**: remove permanently from the game.
- **Seize the Initiative**: take the initiative marker. You can't if you hold it or someone already seized this round.
- **Spend**: return a resource to the supply to use it.
- **Steal**: take from a Rival's play area. Some rules forbid stealing specifically.
- **Supply**: the general supply holds resources and shared pieces; each player's supply holds their ships and agents.
- **Swap**: give one piece and take another at the same time.
- **Take**: add to your play area.
- **Turn**: playing one action card and taking its actions.
- **Turn Order**: start with the initiative holder and go clockwise.

## 11. Leaders & Lore (p.21)

- **Yes, it's in the base box:** 8 Leader cards and 14 Lore cards.
- **Not for a first game.** The rulebook is explicit about this; play a game or two first.
- **What changes:** it replaces setup steps N–O.
  1. Lay out face-up rows of Leaders and Lore cards, each with one more card than there are players.
  2. In **reverse turn order** (starting to the right of the initiative holder, going counterclockwise), each player takes 1 Leader **or** 1 Lore card. Repeat until everyone has 1 of each.
  3. Your **Leader** card replaces the standard starting pieces (ship pips under A/B/C), sets your 2 starting resources, and gives you a unique power.
  4. **Lore** cards give a permanent power. They have no suit or raid cost, so they don't count for ambitions and can't be stolen.
- Optional chaos variant: everyone takes 2 Lore cards.
- For a beginner tutorial, mention it in one line and skip it.

## 12. Turn summary / quick reference (from p.3 and the aid-sheet content, my words)

**One round**
1. **Leader** (initiative holder):
   1. May declare the ambition on the card (that ambition's box gets the highest available marker; the zero marker goes on the card).
   2. Spends any resources in the Prelude.
   3. Takes 1 action per pip of the card's suit.
   4. Or passes: the initiative goes to the next player clockwise who has cards, and the round ends.
2. **Each other player**, clockwise:
   - **Surpass** (same suit, higher number): all pips.
   - **Pivot** (other suit, face up): 1 action from your own card.
   - **Copy** (face down): 1 action from the lead card.
   - May **seize** (with an extra face-down card, or a 7 Surpass in 4-player games) if nobody has seized yet.
3. **Initiative:** whoever seized keeps it. Otherwise it goes to the highest Surpass. Otherwise it stays put.
4. **Discard** all played cards face down. If anyone has cards, start a new round; otherwise the chapter ends.

**End of chapter**
1. Score the declared ambitions.
2. Return Trophies if Warlord scored, and Captives if Tyrant scored.
3. Flip the lowest unflipped marker.
4. Check the game end: 27 / 30 / 33 Power, or chapter 5.
5. Everyone draws 6 cards.

**Actions by suit**
- **Administration**: Tax / Repair / Influence
- **Aggression**: Battle / Move / Secure
- **Construction**: Build / Repair
- **Mobilization**: Move / Influence

**Resources in Prelude**
- Material: Build or Repair
- Fuel: Move
- Weapon: pips may Battle
- Relic: Secure
- Psionic: one lead-card action
