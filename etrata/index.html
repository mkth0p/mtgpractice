Dimir Assassins · face-down theft

DeadlyEtrata

A blue-black Assassin deck that steals the table's cards. Every Assassin that connects cloaks the top card of that player's library under your control. Their creatures fight for you, and Etrata turns their other cards face up or casts them for free. How the deck wins

Hit, cloak, flip, repeat

    An Assassin connects

    Cheap evasive Assassins do the work: Changeling Outcast, Hookblade Veteran, Brotherhood Spy and Basim Ibn Ishaq.

  2. 2
    Etrata cloaks

    Each hit puts the top card of that player's library onto the battlefield under your control, face down as a 2/2 with ward {2}.

  3. 3

    Roshan, Hidden Magister, Maskwood Nexus and Arcane Adaptation make them Assassins, so next turn they hit and cloak too.

    Combos & kills

    How to actually win

    Most games are won by stealing the table's best cards and attacking with more creatures than they can block. When the board stalls, the deck has two two-card combos and two cards that say "loses the game". Ramses, Assassin Lord turns any of those eliminations into a win for the whole table, as long as that player was attacked by one of your Assassins this turn. Tell your table about the combos before you play.

    Combo 1Mill and drainNo. 01 · InstantDuskmantle Guildmage + Mindcrank
    +
    You need
    Both on the battlefield, {1}{U}{B} for Guildmage's first ability, and a starter: any life loss or any card going to an opponent's graveyard.
    Total mana
    7 across two or more turns: Guildmage 2, Mindcrank 2, the ability 3.
    Speed
    Instant. Activate it at the end of an opponent's turn, or during combat.
    1. Pay {1}{U}{B}: for the rest of the turn, whenever a card is put into an opponent's graveyard from anywhere, that player loses 1 life.
    2. Start it: an Assassin hits them, or Guildmage's second ability ({2}{U}{B}) mills two cards.
    3. They lose life. Mindcrank mills that many cards.
    4. Each milled card makes them lose 1 life, which mills again. It stops when they're dead or out of cards.
    Does it hit every opponent?

    Guildmage's ability covers every opponent, but each chain needs its own starter. A combat hit on each player starts all of them at once.

    What stops it?

    Removing Guildmage before its ability resolves, or Mindcrank at any point. Once the ability has resolved, killing Guildmage doesn't stop it for that turn. Dispel and Counterspell protect the setup.

    Speed
    One combat and one end step.
    1. Unstoppable Slasher hits: the player loses its damage, then half their remaining life, rounded up.
    2. At the beginning of the end step, Wound Reflection makes them lose the life they lost this turn again. That's always more than they have left.
    Alt winsLoses the gameNo. 03 · CombatEtrata, the Silencer and Strixhaven Stadium

    Etrata, the Silencer can't be blocked. Each time she deals combat damage to a player, she exiles one of their creatures with a hit counter, and that player loses once they own three exiled cards with hit counters. Then she shuffles into your library, so March of Swirling Mist phasing her out in response keeps her around. Strixhaven Stadium gains a point counter each time one of your creatures hits an opponent; the hit that brings it to ten makes that player lose.

    Pair them with Ramses

    With Ramses, Assassin Lord on the battlefield, a player who loses after being attacked by one of your Assassins this turn wins you the game.

    Game plan

    How to pilot it

    You're the tempo deck at the table: cheap threats, counterspells held up, and a commander that gets better every turn you connect. Stay small and hard to read, take the table's cards, and let the opponents spend their removal on each other.

    Turns 1–3The setup
    • A one- or two-drop Assassin: Changeling Outcast, Hookblade Veteran, Brotherhood Spy.
    • A mana rock if you have no Assassin: Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Dimir Signet.
    • Etrata on turn 3, before combat, with an Assassin ready to hit.
    Turns 4–6The heist
    • Attack every turn with evasive Assassins at the player with the fewest blockers.
    • Add a type enabler: Roshan, Hidden Magister, Maskwood Nexus, Arcane Adaptation.
    • Keep {U} or {U}{U} open for Dispel or Counterspell when the board is worth protecting.
    Turn 7+The kill
    • Flip the stolen bombs with Etrata's {2}{U}{B}.
    • Assemble a combo, or set up Etrata, the Silencer or the Stadium, with Ramses on the battlefield.
    • Toxic Deluge resets a table that got ahead of you.

    Sequencing

    Ten habits that win games

    1. Cast Etrata before combat. Her trigger only counts hits while she's on the battlefield.
    2. Attack every turn. Each Assassin that connects is a free card.
    3. Look at your cloaks. Know what you have before you plan the turn.
    4. Flip creatures for their mana cost. It's cheaper than Etrata's ability and nobody can respond.
    5. Flip Roshan after blockers. Menace and Assassin types arrive when blocks are locked in.
    6. Counter the wipe. Save a counterspell for the spell that clears your board.
    7. Keep a blocker home. Etrata's deathtouch keeps you alive while the cloaks work.
    8. Cast Spark Double, never flip it. It copies nothing when turned face up.
    9. Line up Ramses. Knock a player out on a turn your Assassin attacked them.
    10. Tell the table about the combos. Guildmage and Mindcrank, Slasher and Wound Reflection.

    Matchups

    Reading the table

    ◐Against board wipes

    Hold a counterspell or March of Swirling Mist once your board is worth protecting. Keep one or two cheap Assassins in hand to rebuild.

    ∞Against combo decks

    You're the deck with counterspells. Keep Counterspell, Arcane Denial or An Offer You Can't Refuse for their key spell, and pressure them first.

    ⚔Against big creature decks

    Etrata's deathtouch and your cloaks block well. Cloak their library: big creature decks give you big creatures. Reality Shift and Infernal Grasp handle the one that matters.

    ⚙Against graveyard decks

    Bojuka Bog exiles a graveyard. Cloaking the top of their library keeps those cards out of the graveyard too.

    Strength

    A fast, interactive Bracket 3

    That's "Upgraded" in the official brackets, roughly 6 to 6.5 on the old 1 to 10 scale. Strong at a casual or upgraded-precon table, nowhere near cEDH. This is a judgement from reading the list, not from tested games.

    1casualupgradedoptimizedcEDH 10

    Why it's strong

    • A 3-mana commander that starts stealing cards on turn 4.
    • Four counterspells, spot removal and Toxic Deluge.
    • Two two-card combos and two cards that make a player lose, all turned into a table win by Ramses.

    What holds it back

    • Small creatures: one board wipe undoes several turns of work.
    • No ramp beyond mana rocks, and no tutors.
    • Stolen cards are random: some turns you cloak a land.

    Before you sit down

    Bracket 3 allows two-card combos as long as they aren't early-game ones. Mention Duskmantle Guildmage + Mindcrank and Ramses at the pregame talk, since some tables ban them.

    Your other deck
