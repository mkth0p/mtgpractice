    Combos & kills

    How to actually win

    The deck has three ways to end a game: a wide attack with a pump spell (most games), one of the infinite combos (when the board stalls or you need to win at instant speed), and Halo Fountain's alternate win. Tell your table about the infinite combos before you play: Bracket 3 allows them late in the game, but some groups don't.

    Combo 1Infinite damageNo. 01 · InstantHeliod + Walking Ballista
    +
    You need
    Heliod on the battlefield (it doesn't need to be a creature), Walking Ballista with at least two +1/+1 counters, and {1}{W}.
    Total mana
    7 to 9 across one or more turns: Heliod 3, Ballista 2 to 4, lifelink 2.
    Speed
    Instant. Everything after casting Ballista can happen on an opponent's turn.
    1. Have Heliod, Sun-Crowned out. Cast Walking Ballista with X=2. With Trostani or Soul Warden out, X=1 is enough: Ballista entering gains you life, and Heliod puts a second counter on it.
    2. Pay {1}{W} for Heliod's ability: Walking Ballista gains lifelink until end of turn.
    3. Remove a counter from Ballista: it deals 1 damage to an opponent. It still has a counter left, so it survives.
    4. Lifelink gains you 1 life. Heliod triggers and puts a +1/+1 counter back on Ballista.
    5. Ballista is back where it started. Repeat step 3 until every opponent is dead.
    Why two counters and not one?

    If Ballista has one counter and you remove it, it becomes a 0/0 and dies before the ping resolves. The damage and lifelink still happen, but Heliod's trigger has no Ballista to target, so the loop ends. With two counters, Ballista survives each ping.

    What if they respond?

    If someone tries to kill Ballista in response to the lifelink activation, remove its counters in response and ping anyway. If Heliod gets exiled or bounced mid-loop, you keep whatever damage you already dealt. Heliod's indestructible, so destroy effects do nothing to it.

    How do I find the pieces?

    Finale of Devastation for X=3 puts Heliod directly onto the battlefield. Don't use Finale for Ballista: it enters with zero counters and dies.

    Combo 2Infinite lifeNo. 02 · FreeSpike Feeder + Heliod, Thune or Cleric Class
    +oror
    You need
    Spike Feeder with at least two counters (it enters with two), plus one of the three engines. No extra mana.
    Speed
    Instant, and free. You can do it in response to a board wipe.
    Win with
    Aetherflux Reservoir (50 damage per 50 life), or Archangel of Thune's infinite counters and an attack.
    1. Remove a +1/+1 counter from Spike Feeder: you gain 2 life (3 with Cleric Class).
    2. The engine triggers. Heliod or Cleric Class puts a counter on target creature: pick Spike Feeder. Archangel of Thune puts a counter on every creature you control, including Feeder.
    3. Feeder has its counter back. Repeat as many times as you like.
    4. With Archangel of Thune, every loop also grows your whole team by +1/+1. Stop at any size and attack.
    5. With Aetherflux Reservoir out, pay 50 life for 50 damage to each opponent in turn.
    Why two counters?

    Removing the last counter is part of the cost, so Feeder dies as a 0/0 before the life arrives and the engine has nothing to put the counter back on. Loop with two or more and it survives every time.

    Is infinite life a win on its own?

    No. It makes you nearly impossible to kill by damage, but you still need to finish. The payoffs in this deck are Aetherflux Reservoir, Soul of Eternity (its size is your life total), Lathiel, the Bounteous Dawn (that many counters at end of turn), and Archangel of Thune growing the team.

    What protects it?

    Shalai, Voice of Plenty gives your other creatures hexproof, which covers Feeder, Thune and Ballista. It doesn't cover Heliod while Heliod isn't a creature, or Aetherflux Reservoir (an artifact). Keep Grand Crescendo or Rootborn Defenses up against wipes.

    Alt winFifteen untapsNo. 03 · In combatHalo Fountain: untap fifteen, win the game

    For {W}{W}{W}{W}{W}, tap Halo Fountain and untap fifteen tapped creatures you control, and you win. The creatures need to be tapped first, so the timing is: attack with fifteen or more creatures without vigilance, then activate it right after attackers are declared, before blockers can kill any of them. Convoke (Hour of Reckoning, Dazzling Theater // Prop Room) also taps creatures, and so does Springleaf Drum.

    Watch out

    Intangible Virtue gives your tokens vigilance, so they won't tap when they attack.

    Game plan

    How to pilot it

    You're the midrange deck at the table: faster than the control players, slower than the combo players. Ramp early, build an engine in the midgame, and end it in one big turn. The whole game is about sequencing: lifegain sources before the creatures that trigger them, bait before your best threat, and protection held up before you commit.

    Turns 1–3Soundcheck
    • Play a mana source every turn: Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Nature's Lore.
    • Drop cheap engines early: Soul Warden, Cleric Class, Ajani's Pridemate, Voice of the Blessed.
    • A good turn 3 is Adeline, Resplendent Cathar or, off a mana creature, Trostani.
    Turns 4–6The build
    • Land Trostani, then the payoffs: Archangel of Thune, Hero of Bladehold, Elspeth, Sun's Champion, Cathars' Crusade.
    • Attack with Adeline and Hero every turn, at the player with the fewest blockers.
    • Keep Grand Crescendo or Rootborn Defenses mana open once your board is worth protecting.
    Turn 7+Encore
    • Count lethal with the calculator, then cast the overrun precombat.
    • If the board is stalled, go for a combo: Heliod + Ballista or Spike Feeder.
    • Finale of Devastation at X=10 or more is a Craterhoof by itself.

    Sequencing

    Ten habits that win games

    1. Lifegain first, bodies second. Soul Warden, Trostani and Innkeeper only count creatures that enter after them.
    2. Populate at the end of the opponent's turn. Trostani's ability works at instant speed. Hold the mana, bluff, and still get the token.
    3. Bait before the Angel. Hero of Bladehold and Adeline must be answered. Once they've eaten removal, Archangel of Thune lives longer.
    4. Skip small Nykthos Paragon triggers. It works once per turn and it's optional. Wait for a big gain.
    5. Order Hero of Bladehold's triggers. Make the tokens first, then battle cry, so the Soldiers get +1/+0.
    6. Don't overextend into a wipe. Keep a token maker in hand, or hold Grand Crescendo or Rootborn Defenses.
    7. Clamp your 1/1s. Skullclamp on a Citizen or Soldier is two cards for one mana. Intangible Virtue turns this off.
    8. Hour of Reckoning is yours. It only kills nontoken creatures. When your board is tokens and theirs isn't, it's one-sided.
    9. Save Swords and Path. Use them on combo pieces and commanders, not on the first creature you see.
    10. Announce the combo turn clearly. Walk the table through the loop, state how many times you repeat it, and move on.

    Matchups

    Reading the table

    ◐Against control and board wipes

    Commit two or three threats at a time, not your whole hand. Voice of Resurgence punishes instant-speed removal on your turn, and Grand Crescendo cast at the end of their turn rebuilds a board from nothing. Elenda's Hierophant leaves tokens behind.

    ∞Against combo decks

    You have no counterspells, so your answer is pressure and removal. Attack the combo player early, and save Beast Within, Generous Gift and Swords to Plowshares for their key piece. Walking Ballista can snipe small combo creatures.

    ⚔Against aggro and voltron

    Your life total is a resource. Chump block with tokens, keep gaining, and use Swords to Plowshares or Path to Exile on a suited-up commander. Shalai, Voice of Plenty stops targeted burn at your face.

    ⚙Against artifacts and enchantments

    Break Down, Sundering Growth and Excavation Technique handle rocks, equipment and stax pieces. Sundering Growth also populates.

      Strength

      A solid Bracket 3

      That's "Upgraded" in the official brackets, roughly 6.5 to 7 on the old 1 to 10 scale. Strong at a casual or upgraded-precon table, nowhere near cEDH. This is a judgement from reading the list, not from tested games.

      1casualupgradedoptimizedcEDH 10

      Why it's strong

      • Two compact infinite combos, and Finale of Devastation can fetch the pieces.
      • Five single-card finishers once a board is out.
      • Real ramp: Sol Ring, two Signets, four mana creatures and three land-ramp spells.

      What holds it back

      • A 4-mana commander that isn't a threat alone. Games end around turn 7 to 9.
      • Light interaction and no counterspells, so fast combo decks can race it.
      • Thin card draw: mostly Skullclamp, Camaraderie and Shamanic Revelation.

      Before you sit down

      Bracket 3 allows two-card infinite combos as long as they aren't early-game ones, which fits here. Mention Heliod + Walking Ballista at the pregame talk, since some tables ban any infinite. Game Changers limits change over time, so check the current official list against this deck before a bracketed event.

      Your other deck

      Miku vs Etrata

      They're close, and both are Bracket 3. The upgraded Miku deck is slightly ahead at about 6.5 to 7, with your Etrata, Deadly Fugitive deck at about 6 to 6.5. Miku hits harder once it's set up. Etrata is faster, more interactive, and would give Miku a hard time head to head. This comes from reading both lists, not from playtesting.

      Finishers

      Craterhoof, Overwhelming Stampede, Triumph of the Hordes and Beastmaster Ascension each end the game by themselves once you have a board. Etrata's closers (Wound Reflection, Unstoppable Slasher, Strixhaven Stadium) need a few turns of hitting first.

      Ramp

      Miku has mana creatures, land-ramp spells and rocks. Etrata runs rocks only.

      Recovering from a wipe

      Trostani, Elspeth and the token makers rebuild quickly.

      Speed

      Etrata costs 3 mana instead of 4 and starts cloaking cards from turn 4. Trostani does little until turn 5 or 6.

      Interaction

      Etrata runs 4 counterspells, spot removal and Toxic Deluge. Miku has no counterspells and only one wrath.

      Card flow

      Gix, Mask of Memory, Key to the City and Night's Whisper give Etrata much more card draw than Miku, which relies mostly on Skullclamp.

      Evasion

      Etrata's assassins get past blockers. Miku mostly has to swing into boards.

      Combos

      About even. Mindcrank + Duskmantle Guildmage and Heliod + Walking Ballista each take about 7 mana across two or three turns. Ramses adds a second win condition to Etrata on top of that.
