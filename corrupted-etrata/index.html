Dimir theft aggro · Bracket 4

CorruptedEtrata

The dark side of the Etrata deck: Etrata, Deadly Fugitive at the head of the heist closer, a Bracket 4 theft-aggro list. Cheap evasive Assassins on turns 1 and 2, Etrata the turn one of them connects, and every hit steals the top card of a library as a face-down 2/2 that attacks for you next turn. Ramses, Assassin Lord is the kill: once an Assassin of yours has attacked a player, their death wins you the game. The vampire drain loop is the second kill. Here's the full manual: a guide in steps, every card with rulings, both kills, a checklist to play with, a quiz to learn it fast, and a real game to practise against.

How the deck wins

Sneak in, steal, snowball, kill one

  1. 1
    Cheap evasive Assassins

    Changeling Outcast, Hired Poisoner, Slither Blade, Mothdust Changeling and Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive on turns 1 and 2, with Sol Ring, the Moxes, Dark Ritual and Ancient Tomb behind them.

  2. 2
    Etrata when one connects

    Her trigger works the turn she's cast. Each Assassin hit cloaks the top card of that player's library: a face-down 2/2 with ward {2}, yours, that attacks next turn.

  3. 3
    The snowball

    Leyline of Transformation, Arcane Adaptation or Roshan, Hidden Magister make the stolen 2/2s Assassins, so they steal too. They Came from the Pipes and Satoru, the Infiltrator turn the steals into cards.

  4. 4
    Kill one player

    Ramses, Assassin Lord turns one death into the game, and the drain loop kills the table. See the kills.

    Kills & how to get through

    How to actually win

    Two kills. Ramses, Assassin Lord turns one dead player into the game, and the drain loop kills the whole table. Tutor for Ramses first, pick one player, and send everything that gets through at them. Tell your table about Ramses and the two-card loop before you play.

    Kill 1One death is the gameNo. 01 · Your turn, after an attackRamses' verdict
    +oror+
    You need
    Ramses on the battlefield when a player loses, and an Assassin you controlled attacked that player this turn.
    Speed
    Your turn, in combat or after it. Any way they lose counts.
    1. Pick the mark: the player your board kills soonest. Declare your Assassins as attackers against that player (not their planeswalker). Attacked is enough: the Assassin doesn't have to hit or survive.
    2. Virtus the Veiled, Unstoppable Slasher or a creature with Quietus Spike deals combat damage: that player loses half their life, rounded up.
    3. Bloodletter of Aclazotz: on your turn, opponents lose twice as much life as they would. Half doubled is all of it.
    4. That player loses the game. They were attacked by an Assassin you controlled this turn, so Ramses' trigger wins you the game.

    The halver is a shortcut, not a requirement

    Any death after the attack wins: combat damage, the drain loop, Vein Ripper. Ramses can even arrive late: cast him in your second main phase and a death after that still counts. In bot games his trigger made the last kill in 50% of the wins against the precons and 46% against Bracket 4.

    Kill 2Drain the tableNo. 02 · Two cards, four pairsThe drain loop
    or+or
    You need
    One of each side on the battlefield, then any opponent losing life or you gaining life.
    Speed
    Any turn. No mana once both are out.
    1. An opponent loses life: your attack, Vein Ripper, Ashnod's Altar sacrificing a stolen 2/2 to Vein Ripper, their own fetch land or shock land.
    2. Exquisite Blood or Bloodthirsty Conqueror: you gain that much life.
    3. Sanguine Bond or Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose: target opponent loses that much life.
    4. That life loss triggers the drain card again. It loops until every opponent is at 0.

    It wins without Ramses

    It's the only other card pair in the deck that kills three players at once. In bot games it made 36% of the kills against the precons, and the deck still won 38% of the games where Ramses never landed (the list without the loop: 17%). With Ramses out, the first player it kills after your attack already wins the game.

    ReachOne hit, all their lifeNo. 03 · Your turnHalf, doubled: Bloodletter + a halver
    +oror
    1. Bloodletter of Aclazotz: during your turn, an opponent who would lose life loses twice that much instead.
    2. The halver deals combat damage, doubled. Then its trigger: half their life, rounded up, counted when it resolves, after the damage. Doubled, that's all of it.
    3. It kills the player it hits, not the table. Without Ramses that's one player; with him, it's the game.

    Only on your turn

    Bloodletter does nothing on an opponent's turn. Without it, a halver takes half: two hits on the same player in one combat still leave them at 1 or more.

    SupportPast the blockers and the wrathsNo. 04 · Evasion and survivalGetting through
    ·····
    1. Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive: your creatures with power or toughness 1 or less can't be blocked. Rogue's Passage ({4}, {T}) does it for one creature of any size.
    2. Reverse the Polarity: creatures can't be blocked this turn, or, in the other mode, counter all other spells.
    3. Eldrazi Monument: +1/+1, flying and indestructible for the whole team. Each upkeep, sacrifice a creature (a stolen 2/2 is fine) or the Monument.
    4. Teferi's Veil: each creature that attacks phases out at end of combat and phases back in at your next untap, so the wraths cast on other players' turns miss it. Once it's out, attack with everything that gets through.
    5. Kindred Dominance: name Assassin and every other creature dies. With a type-changer out, the stolen 2/2s are Assassins and stay.

    Pumps break Tetsuko

    Tetsuko checks power and toughness as blockers are declared. Once Ramses or Achilles Davenport gives your Assassins +1/+1, Virtus and the other 1/1s are 2/2 and can be blocked. Changeling Outcast and Slither Blade don't care: their own text does it.

    Strength

    A mid Bracket 4 aggro deck

    Bracket 4 is "Optimized": no limit on Game Changers, two-card combos or tutors, but not built for the cEDH tournament meta. This deck plays 9 Game Changers and none of its cards is banned. In 5,040 four-player bot games against three precons it won 47.9%, and 27.5% against three Bracket 4 bots, with an average winning round of 9.0 and 8.0. These are bot games: humans at a Bracket 4 table kill Ramses faster and hold up more interaction, so expect less at a real table. The order of the lists and the piloting rules are what carries over.

    1casualupgradedoptimizedcEDH 10

    Why it's strong

    • Nine evasive Assassins and changelings cost one or two mana, and seven pieces of fast mana plus Ancient Tomb put them down early.
    • Etrata steals: 7.3 cards a game against the precons and 5.1 against Bracket 4, in bot games. Without her the deck won 16.7 points less against the precons.
    • One death is the game once Ramses, Assassin Lord is out, and eight tutors find him. With him the deck won 56% / 44%.
    • The drain loop is a second kill that doesn't need Ramses: 38% of the games without him still won against the precons (17% for the list without the loop).
    • Teferi's Veil, Eldrazi Monument and Kindred Dominance keep the board alive through wraths.

    What holds it back

    • It leans on Ramses. He landed in 54% / 36% of bot games; without him it won 38% / 18%.
    • Wraths on their owners' turns are what removed Ramses most often in the bot games, and a bigger pile of face-down 2/2s loses more to them.
    • A deck of 1/1s and 2/2s runs into blockers. Pumps from Ramses and Achilles Davenport turn off Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive.
    • Demonic Consultation and the draw engines can deck you: 8% of the losses against the precons were an empty library.

    Game Changers in the deck (9)

    Imperial Seal, Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Rhystic Study, Fierce Guardianship, Force of Will, Cyclonic Rift, Chrome Mox and Ancient Tomb. The brackets are still in beta, so check the official list before a game.

    Your other decks

    Corrupted Etrata vs Etrata

    Same commander, and this time the same idea. The Etrata deck is a Bracket 3 Assassin deck that cloaks and steals through combat, with a Bracket 4 aggro upgrade. The heist closer is that plan built on a Bracket 4 shell: more fast mana, more tutors, free counters, Ramses as the kill and the drain loop behind it. In the same bot engine the Etrata Bracket 4 aggro list won 25.4% against three precons and 14.7% against three Bracket 4 bots; the heist closer won 47.9% and 27.5%. The v3 drain list this site used to document won more (68.0% / 41.7%), but almost all of it from the combo, not from Etrata. Don't bring the heist to a Bracket 3 table. 43 of its 100 cards come from the Etrata deck, basics included. These are bot games, not playtesting: humans kill Ramses faster.
