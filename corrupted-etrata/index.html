Dimir theft and odd combos · Bracket 4

CorruptedEtrata

The dark side of the Etrata deck: Etrata, Deadly Fugitive at the head of "Etrata's Shadow Market", a Bracket 4 list built to win on turns 6 to 7 with cards almost nobody plays. A vampire court that drains the table, cheap deathtouch blockers, infinite turns with Wormfang Manta, and a lot of stealing. Here's the full manual: a guide in steps, every card with rulings, the four win lines, a checklist to play with, a quiz to learn it fast, and a real game to practise against.

    Combos & kills

    How to actually win

    Four lines, two of them two cards. Go for the one you're closest to, keep a tutor for the missing piece, and protect the turn you go off with Fierce Guardianship, Swan Song or Counterspell. Tell your table about the infinite combos and the infinite turns before you play.

    Line 1Drain the tableNo. 01 · Two cards, ten pairsThe vampire court loop
    or+orororor
    You need
    One drain card and one payoff on the battlefield, then any opponent losing life or you gaining life.
    Speed
    Any time an opponent loses life, on any turn. No mana once both are out.
    1. An opponent loses life: your attack, a Hooded Blightfang trigger, their fetch land or shock land, Mindcrank's Guildmage, anything. Your own life gain, like Vampire of the Dire Moon's lifelink, starts it too.
    2. Exquisite Blood or Bloodthirsty Conqueror: you gain that much life.
    3. Marauding Blight-Priest or Starscape Cleric makes each opponent lose 1; Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose, Sanguine Bond or Enduring Tenacity makes one opponent lose that much.
    4. That life loss triggers the drain card again. It loops until every opponent is at 0.

    Underplayed on purpose

    Starscape Cleric costs 2, Blight-Priest and Vito cost 3, and few tables see them coming. Enduring Tenacity comes back as an enchantment when it dies as a creature. Vito has 1 power, so Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive makes him unblockable too.

    Line 2They mill themselves to deathNo. 02 · Instant speedMindcrank + Duskmantle Guildmage
    +
    You need
    Both out, and {1}{U}{B} for the Guildmage's ability.
    Speed
    Instant. Best on an opponent's turn, when they cast a spell.
    1. Pay {1}{U}{B}: this turn, whenever a card is put into an opponent's graveyard from anywhere, that player loses 1 life.
    2. Start it: an opponent's spell resolving, a mill, or Duskmantle Guildmage's other ability ({2}{U}{B}: target player mills two cards).
    3. Mindcrank: whenever an opponent loses life, they mill that many cards. Each milled card costs them 1 life, which mills another card.
    4. It runs until that opponent has no library and no life left. Each loop kills one player, so every other opponent needs their own starter: a Windfall, a Toxic Deluge, an attack.

    Finding the pieces

    Tribute Mage finds Mindcrank. Shred Memory and Muddle the Mixture transmute for either piece (both have mana value 2).

    Line 3One hit, all their lifeNo. 03 · Your turnDouble tap: Bloodletter + Virtus
    ++
    1. Bloodletter of Aclazotz: on your turn, opponents lose twice as much life as they would.
    2. Virtus the Veiled deals combat damage: that player loses half their life, rounded up. Doubled, that's all of it.
    3. Tetsuko Umezawa, Fugitive makes Virtus, a 1/1, unblockable. Rogue's Passage ({4}, {T}) does it too.
    4. It kills the player Virtus hits, not the table. Finish the others with another line, or use it on the player closest to winning.

    Keep Virtus a 1/1

    Tetsuko checks power and toughness as blockers are declared. If anything makes Virtus 2/2 or bigger, it can be blocked: use Rogue's Passage instead, or attack where nobody can block it.

    Line 4An extra turn every turnNo. 04 · 5 mana a turnScroll of Fate + Wormfang Manta + Crystal Shard
    ++
    1. Manifest Wormfang Manta from your hand with Scroll of Fate ({T}). It enters face down, so its "skip your next turn" trigger never happens.
    2. Etrata turns it face up for {2}{U}{B} instead of {5}{U}{U}. Turning face up isn't entering, so still no skipped turn.
    3. Crystal Shard ({U}, {T}) returns it to your hand. When it leaves the battlefield, you take an extra turn.
    4. Next turn, manifest it again. That's 5 mana a turn, 3 with Training Grounds, for infinite turns.

    In the game

    The Play tab plays this line as written: a manifested Manta has no abilities, a flipped one gives you an extra turn when it leaves, and casting it normally makes you skip your next turn.

    Strength

    A mid to high Bracket 4 deck

    Bracket 4 is "Optimized": no limit on Game Changers, two-card combos or tutors, but not built for the cEDH tournament meta. This deck plays 9 Game Changers and none of its cards is banned. It has no Thassa's Oracle and no Mana Vault, so it is slower than the Etrata Heist list. In 8,000 simulated games against opponents who answer 30% of combo attempts, it won 25% of games by turn 6, 40% by turn 7 and 52% by turn 8 (32%, 50% and 64% when nobody answers). The sim hardly counts stolen cards or combat damage, so treat those as a floor.

    1casualupgradedoptimizedcEDH 10

    Why it's strong

    • Four win lines, three of them two cards, and 14 tutors to find them.
    • The vampire loop has ten possible pairs, and any life loss starts it. In paired bot games, the v3 list won about 66.6% against precons (v2: 49.7%) and 39.1% against Bracket 4 decks (v2: 24.8%).
    • Cheap deathtouch blockers and a face-down Silumgar Assassin keep you alive while you set up.
    • Etrata's flip makes Wormfang Manta cost 4 instead of 7.
    • Stealing and Necropotence keep your hand full while theirs empties.

    What holds it back

    • Only four counterspells to protect a combo turn.
    • Rest in Peace stops the Mindcrank loop, and Null Rod stops Scroll of Fate, Crystal Shard and your mana rocks.
    • Wishclaw Talisman and Scheming Symmetry hand an opponent a tutor.
    • Many lines go through creatures, so board wipes slow it down.

    Game Changers in the deck (9)

    Necropotence, Imperial Seal, Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Opposition Agent, Notion Thief, Rhystic Study, Fierce Guardianship and Cyclonic Rift. The brackets are still in beta, so check the official list before a game.

    Your other decks

    Corrupted Etrata vs Etrata

    Same commander, two very different decks. The Etrata deck is a Bracket 3 Assassin deck that cloaks and steals through combat, with a Bracket 4 aggro upgrade. Corrupted Etrata keeps the cloaks and the stealing but wins with odd combos and tutors, usually on turns 6 to 8. Don't bring it to a Bracket 3 table. It shares 45 of its 100 cards with the Etrata deck, basics included. This comes from the lists and the sim, not from playtesting.

    Build it

    From your Etrata deck, plus 55 proxies

    You build this one from your current Etrata deck: 45 of the 100 cards (Etrata, the Mindcrank pieces, Scroll of Fate, Training Grounds, the rocks, most of the lands and 17 basics) come straight out of it. The other 55 are proxies. Real copies of those would cost about $1,070, so proxying saves almost the whole price. Tell your table they're proxies before you play.
